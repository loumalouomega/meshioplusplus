# meshio++ roadmap

Status at time of writing: **v19.0.0**, ABI 39. What ships is listed where it is documented rather than counted here, where counts went stale: the formats in [supported formats](./formats.md) and the [conformance matrix](./conformance.md), the operations in [architecture](./architecture.md#the-operations-layer), the language surfaces in the [C API](./c_api.md), [Fortran](./fortran.md), [Julia](./julia.md), [R](./r.md) and [WebAssembly](./wasm.md) pages.

This document lists what is *not* built. Nothing here duplicates shipped functionality; where a feature partially exists, the shipped half is named and the gap is stated explicitly. Release history lives in [`CHANGELOG.md`](https://github.com/loumalouomega/meshioplusplus/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md), not here.

Effort key: **S** = days, **M** = a couple of weeks, **L** = a month or more, **XL** = a project in its own right. An item names a **probe** — the failing test that proves the gap — wherever one is cheap.

## How this file works

- **Sections are ordered, and the order is the recommendation.** Each section states what belongs in it; an item that sounds exciting does not move up for that reason. An empty section is removed, not kept as a placeholder.
- **A closed item is removed, not struck through.** Its history is the `CHANGELOG.md` entry and the feature's own `doc/` page; a partly closed item is narrowed to what remains (the `AGENTS.md` change checklist rule).
- **Every level of the outline is numbered**, so a gap can be cited in an issue, a commit or a release note: a group of work is `section.item` (a `###`/`####` heading, or a bold label line), the work under it `section.item.subitem`, and a bullet under that `section.item.subitem.subsub`. Lists that record a decision rather than open work — *Considered, not queued*, a section's *Landing an item* steps and the [non-goals](#non-goals-and-decisions-taken) — stay unnumbered, because a number here means work to do. The numbering follows the sections, so a new section inserted at the top renumbers the rest, as the rule above says, and so does a section removed once its last item closes (Performance, October 2026: what was §4 onward moved up one; and again when reader and writer parity closed, the same month: operations, ecosystem reach and the full WebAssembly build became §3 to §5). The long-run section closed the same month as the last of its spikes landed ([findings](./spikes.md)); being last, it renumbered nothing. Mesh generation (§6) closed the same month, also last: its native items landed, its measured-and-parked notes moved to [mesh generation](./mesh_cad.md#measured-and-parked) with their numbers and its references to [mesh generation's further reading](./mesh_cad.md#further-reading), its decisions to the [non-goals](#non-goals-and-decisions-taken), and its remaining dependencies to the sections they wait on (2.3, 3.3.1, 3.4), then 2.3, 3.2.1 and 3.3. Since then a heading other pages link to carries an explicit anchor without its number (`{#awaiting-a-licensed-run}`, `{#quality-of-implementation}`), so a renumbering no longer breaks those links: link to the anchor, and cite the number in the text.
- **Defect-shaped items go in a *Correctness debts* section at the top, regardless of size** — behaviour that loses data, mis-orients cells, does not terminate on valid input or fails silently is not a feature request, even when the fix and the feature are the same work. The section is removed when its last item closes, which renumbers the rest, and the next defect found opens it again as §1.
- **An item estimated from a doc, a `.d.ts` or a changelog alone says so** ("verify first") and names its probe; the code has repeatedly been more or less capable than its description.
- **[Non-goals](#non-goals-and-decisions-taken) record decisions already taken**, with their reasons, so they are not re-proposed as gaps.

## 1. Correctness debts {#correctness-debts}

*Admission: behaviour that loses data, mis-orients cells, does not terminate on valid input or fails silently, whatever the size of the fix. Both items were found while auditing the ParaView plugin for [§6](#paraview-plugin) (November 2026), and both close with its payload layer (6.1).*

- **1.1 The ParaView plugin draws linear wedges inside out.** meshio++ stores a linear `wedge` in Gmsh's node order, whose (0, 1, 2) triangle faces inward, and VTK needs the permutation `[0, 2, 1, 3, 5, 4]` (`_vtk_common.meshio_to_vtk_order`). The plugin's reader hands each block's rows to `SetCells` unchanged (`tools/paraview-meshioplusplus-plugin.py`, `MeshioReader.RequestData`), so every wedge ParaView shows has a negative volume; its writer takes VTK's rows back without the inverse, so a wedge mesh made or edited in ParaView is written inverted. A read followed by a write cancels the two, which is why the smoke test (`tests/python/test_paraview_plugin.py`, tetrahedra and triangles only) passes. The fix is the payload `_interop._to_vtk_payload` already builds for `to_pyvista`, which permutes wedges, on the way in, and `_vtk_common.vtk_cells_from_data`, which applies the inverse, on the way out. Probe: read `tests/python/meshes/mfem/toroid-wedge.mesh` through the plugin and require every volume ParaView's `CellSize` filter reports to be positive; write a ParaView wedge and require `quality` to find no inverted cell. **S**
- **1.2 The ParaView plugin drops `polygonN` blocks and 1-D points.** A block of uniform n-gons is named `polygon3`, `polygon5`, ... (the boundary blocks the OpenFOAM, Fluent and CGNS readers produce), and the reader looks the name up in `meshio_to_vtk_type`, which knows only `polygon`: the block is skipped with one line on standard error, and its cell data with it. The reader also pads only two-column points to three, so a 1-D mesh reaches `SetPoints` with one column. `_interop._vtk_type_id` maps every `polygon*` name to `VTK_POLYGON` and `_to_vtk_payload` pads points of any width, so the fix of 1.1 closes this too. Probe: a mesh with a `polygon5` block, and a 1-D line mesh, read through the plugin with the cell counts `meshioplusplus.read` gives. **S**

## 2. Format reach {#format-reach}

*Admission: a format a simulation or physics-ML workflow actually exchanges; a shipped format's missing half is not a roadmap item, because every registered format reads and writes ([decisions](#non-goals-and-decisions-taken)). "Exchanges" means a file that crosses a tool boundary in a real pipeline (mesher → solver, solver → post-processor, solver → training set), not a format that merely exists. Every item names the consumer on the other side of the file.*

Sizes: **S** ≈ days, **M** ≈ two weeks, **L** ≈ a month or more, including tests, docs page and CLI wiring. They assume a new format reuses the shared components rather than growing its own: the card tokenizer (`detail/keyword_card.hpp`), the [node-ordering registry](./node_ordering.md), the Fortran record reader (`detail/fortran_records.hpp`) and the directory sniffing that sequence globs share.

Link legend: unmarked links were opened or returned by a search while this section was written (September 2026); links marked † are quoted from memory and must be checked before relying on them. Vendor documentation portals move often — when a link is dead, search the document title.

---

Every format this section queued is now read: the native FEM interchange, solver input and result-file readers of v16.5.0 to v16.12.0 (Tecplot, Femap, MFEM, Patran, Abaqus `.fil`, Radioss, Z88, libMesh, Marc, Ansys `.rst`, LS-DYNA and OP2), DOLFINx's ADIOS2 `.bp` ([`vtx`](./formats/vtx.md)) and Tecplot SZL through TecIO ([`szplt`](./formats/szplt.md)) of v16.13.0, and the [vendor-runtime routes](./vendor_routes.md) for the files only a vendor's software reads (Abaqus `.odb`, Marc `.t16`, Femap `.modfem`, Ansys results beyond the native reader). Since v16.17.0 the shared components are used wherever a format's layout fits them, the input decks are written too (Radioss's starter deck, checked with the OpenRadioss starter, and Marc's), Femap writes a series as one output set per step, and every format read but not written names its reason on the [conformance page](./conformance.md#formats-missing-a-direction), which a test keeps in step with the registry; the missing half of each has since been written, so every registered format reads and writes. What they still lack is a check against a run of the vendor tool, listed under [Awaiting a licensed run](#awaiting-a-licensed-run): take each as soon as a licence, or a file the tool wrote, is in hand.

### Considered, not queued

- **Graphics, CAD, VFX or lab-specific rather than simulation interchange:** 3MF, X3D, Alembic, OpenVDB, Silo, LAS/LAZ, E57, OBJ materials (`.mtl`). DXF, once listed here, is reopened as 7.6.4, for sketches and outlines measured from a scan.
- **Closed FEM databases with no route worth shipping:** Altair `.h3d` (undisclosed format, SDK not redistributable — Altair's own advice for OpenRadioss is ANIM → VTK, see the [OpenRadioss discussion](https://github.com/orgs/OpenRadioss/discussions/552)); HyperMesh `.hm`; Abaqus `.sim`.
- **No native format to implement:** deal.II (reads and writes UCD, VTK, Gmsh, Abaqus, Exodus — all reachable today); MOOSE (Exodus).
- **Nastran OP4 matrices and punch `.pch`:** matrix and card dumps, not mesh/field exchange; pyNastran serves them.
- **Non-MSC Nastran HDF5 dialects:** incompatible schemas; revisit with a file in hand.
- **Partitioning niche:** gmsh `$PartitionedEntities` (Kratos partitions through MDPA).
- **CalculiX `.dat` print files:** `frd.read_dat` stays a Python helper that returns a list of table dictionaries; it is not a `Mesh`, it has no writer, and `.dat` already belongs to Tecplot and Marc ([CalculiX results](./formats/frd.md)).
- **Operators, not formats** (listed only because they came up in the same review): mesh duals other than the tetrahedral median dual (now [`tet_to_poly`](./tet_poly.md)). ICP registration, the cylindrical and spherical transforms and the shell completions, listed here with them, shipped as [`register_rigid`](./registration.md), [`convert_coordinates`](./coordinates.md) and `meshioplusplus completion`.

Revisit any of them when a consumer asks with a file in hand — a real deck or result file, the tool version that wrote it, and the tool that needs the converted output.

### 2.2 Awaiting a licensed run {#awaiting-a-licensed-run}

Checks that need the vendor tool itself, or a file it wrote that no public source has. Each reader works from the vendor's documentation and every public file found (September 2026); what remains is one probe per line.

The [vendor-runtime routes](./vendor_routes.md) of v16.13.0 are tested against stand-ins of the vendor APIs only. Each needs one recorded run, with the vendor version written into its page; what to install for it:

- **2.2.1 Abaqus `.odb`.** Abaqus 2020 or later (its `abaqus python`, whose bundled numpy the script needs; 2023 and before run Python 2.7, 2024 and later Python 3). Run `contrib/abaqus_odb/odb_to_vtu.py` on a job with shells, C3D20R and an assembly-level set, and compare its nodal `U` and centroidal `S` against the same job's `.fil` read natively ([route](./routes/abaqus_odb.md)).
- **2.2.2 Marc `.t16`.** Marc/Mentat 2020 or later with PyPost (`py_post`, run with Mentat's Python). Run `contrib/marc_t16/t16_to_vtu.py` on a job that writes both `.t16` and `.t19`, and compare against the `.t19` read natively; confirm that post file position 0 holds the model ([route](./routes/marc_t16.md)).
- **2.2.3 Ansys results via PyDPF.** A DPF Server (Ansys 2021 R1 or later, or the standalone DPF Server 2024 R2 or later) and `pip install ansys-dpf-core pyvista meshioplusplus[h5py]`. Run `contrib/ansys_dpf/rst_to_vtkhdf.py` on a structural `.rst` and compare against the native `.rst` reader; then on a `/FCOMP,RST,1` file, which the native reader refuses ([route](./routes/ansys_dpf.md)).
- **2.2.4 Femap `.modfem`.** Femap 2301 or later on Windows, and `pip install pywin32`. Run `contrib/femap/export_neutral.py`, check `feFileWriteNeutral`'s argument list against that version's API help, and read the neutral file back ([route](./routes/femap_modfem.md)).
- **2.2.5 Tecplot `.szplt`.** Tecplot 360 2023 or later, and `pip install pytecplot`. Save a data set as `.szplt` and as `.plt` from Tecplot itself and read both (the TecIO-backed reader against the native one); run `contrib/tecplot/szplt_to_plt.py` and the macro `contrib/tecplot/szplt_to_plt.mcr`; and point `MESHIOPLUSPLUS_TECIO_LIBRARY` at Tecplot's own shared `libtecio` for the ctypes reader ([route](./routes/tecplot_szplt.md)). Also open in Tecplot 360 a file meshio++ wrote with single-precision, 16-bit and byte variables and one with partitioned zones; and, with a TecIO newer than 2018.3 (which refuses to write polygon and polyhedral zones in an SZL file), write such a zone, read it back, and check the written face map against Tecplot's reading, which would turn the refusal of those zones into a reader and a writer.

The readers:

- **2.2.6 Tecplot.** A `.plt` written by `preplot` from the ASCII fixtures (`#!TDV112`), read identically to its `.dat`, and the `.plt` meshio++ now writes opened in Tecplot or checked by `preplot`; binary files of versions 100–105 and 109–111, of which no sample was found (the VisIt suite covers 71, 75, 106–108).
- **2.2.7 Femap.** Element (404) and group (408) records written by Femap 10 and by 12 or later (the public 2401 files hold none); the slot layout of the 13-node pyramid (topology 19); a file meshio++ writes, a multi-set series included, imported into Femap.
- **2.2.8 Patran.** A neutral file exported by ANSA or HyperMesh.
- **2.2.9 Abaqus `.fil`.** A binary run with shells and C3D20R, checked against its `.dat` printout (the truss run TenBarArea is checked); a binary file of a single-precision build (4-byte words), of which no layout description or sample was found, which decides whether the reader and the writer take that width; and a file meshio++ writes, opened in Abaqus.
- **2.2.10 Radioss.** Animation files of the layouts before OpenRadioss (magic numbers `0x5426` to `0x542B`): neither OpenRadioss's tools nor its readers document them. The 16-bit encoding of the nodal normals (the writer leaves them zero: `anim_to_vtk` ignores them and the encoding is undocumented), and a run of OpenRadioss's `anim_to_vtk` on a file meshio++ wrote, time-history lists included.
- **2.2.11 Marc.** A run of Marc itself, including on a deck or a `.t19` post file (a remeshing series included) meshio++ writes (`SIZING`'s fields, one type per `ELEMENTS` line, and the shell and plane-strain default types follow Volume C and Mentat's decks); post files before revision 9 (Marc K7 and earlier, Volume D 2000's PLDUMP layout); Volume A's edge and face numbering, to map edge and face sets (kept as `(cell, number)` field data) onto facets, which also decides whether side regions can be written to decks and post files; the binary `.t16`, read or written natively, which needs Volume D's PLDUMP description of it or a sample file (today it is read only through Marc's PyPost, by the [route](./routes/marc_t16.md)).
- **2.2.12 Ansys `.rst`.** A file written with `/FCOMP,RST,1` or higher (zlib records): `detail/zlib_inflate` is ready, the record framing needs a sample. A harmonic (complex) result file, which no public sample or open reader covers (pymapdl-reader takes cyclic mode pairs only and only DPF reads complex sets), so complex results are neither read nor written; and a file meshio++ writes with element rotations and nodal coordinate systems, read by DPF (pymapdl-reader applies the three nodal rotations in the opposite order, so it agrees only on single-axis angles).
- **2.2.13 LS-DYNA.** A family from LS-DYNA with adaptive remeshing (`NADAPT`), the `d3thdt` time-history database, the cubic and bubble solids (21P, 15T, 20T, 40P, 64), airbag particles and rigid roads (read and written from the manual and lasso-python only: the airbag variable names of a real file, and rigid roads with the full rigid-body velocities, `NDIM = 9`, which the writer leaves out), a real `d3part`, thick shells, and the whole reader against LS-PrePost, with the families and `binout` files (links included) meshio++ writes opened there; whether LS-DYNA writes a ten-node solid's extra nodes once or twice.
- **2.2.14 Nastran OP2.** Random element tables, complex SORT2 nodal tables, CBUSH and CGAP forces, CBUSH real stresses, and NX's solid types 300–303 (read from pyNastran's layouts; no sample). Files meshio++ writes, opened in Femap, Patran or an NX/Simcenter post-processor: the writer produces the MSC dialect only, and the element tables it writes beyond pyNastran's reach (the SORT1 PSD, CRM and ATO random tables, the complex layouts pyNastran does not read) follow the reader's layouts.
- **2.2.15 CalculiX `frd`.** `cgx -b` (CalculiX GraphiX) reading a binary file meshio++ writes; `ccx2paraview` parses the ASCII layout only, so no open tool checks the binary variant beyond meshio++'s own reader and the binary files `ccx` wrote.
- **2.2.16 FEBio `.xplt`.** FEBio Studio or `febio4` opening a file meshio++ wrote: the remeshed series (a mesh section per remeshed step), surfaces with per-surface-node variables, and the compressed states.
- **2.2.17 Nastran HDF5.** MSC's own post-processors (Patran, Simcenter) reading a file meshio++ wrote: its tables follow the column sets of the six MSC Nastran 2020 files, but the `CBUSH`, `CGAP`, `CMASS` and `CONM1` element tables and the `MAT5`, `MAT8`, `MAT9`, `PBARL`, `PBEAML` and `PCOMPG` tables have no sample, so they are not written; a file with them would add them.
- **2.2.18 DOLFINx VTX.** ParaView's `ADIOS2VTXReader` (`tools/gen_vtx_reference.py` drives it) and DOLFINx reading a file meshio++ wrote with several cell types or parts (one block per type and part, with `meshio.shared_points`): both read one cell type per file, so this is meshio++'s own extension of the layout; and a `VTXMeshPolicy.reuse` file (ParaView 6.1 aborts on every step after the first of DOLFINx's own).

## 3. Quality of implementation {#quality-of-implementation}

*Admission: work that makes every other item safer to land. None of it is a feature, so none of it competes for the same attention — it can run in parallel with everything.*

The sanitizer leg, the reader fuzzing, the format conformance matrix, the property tests, the benchmark harness, the ParaView plugin test and the fallback narrowing all shipped in v16.14.0 ([fuzzing and sanitizers](./fuzzing.md), [format conformance](./conformance.md), [benchmarks](./benchmarks.md)). ## 5. Performance {#performance}

*Admission: a slowdown in a path a user hits, measured or verified in the code, with the shape of the fix named. Every item lands through [landing a performance change](./benchmarks.md#landing-a-performance-change): output byte-identical across backends, thread counts and the baseline digests, SEQ not slower, the ABI tier stated. The section closed in October 2026 with its decisions recorded in the [non-goals](#non-goals-and-decisions-taken); the November 2026 audit reopened it with what those decisions do not cover, and re-proposes none of them except 5.3.2, whose stated reason the code no longer supports. The impact estimates come from reading the code and from the tier rows of [benchmarks](./benchmarks.md) and the [XL spike](./spikes.md), not from new measurements: each item's first step is the measurement its probe names.*

- **5.1 The harness first.** The items below are only provable where the harness sees them. The pull-request gate on instruction counts landed ([benchmarks](./benchmarks.md#instruction-count-gate)). **S–M**
  - **5.1.2 The missing rows.** Formats: the result-file readers (`lsdyna_d3plot`, `nastran_op2`, `ansys_rst`, `xplt`). Integrations: the WASM ingestion of a JS mesh, the physics-ML Python layers, and the ParaView and Blender converters. An item below whose operation has no row adds one first, as the landing rules require.
  - **5.1.4 `decimate_volume`'s ballooning star.** Its row runs at tier S only ([benchmarks](./benchmarks.md#decimate_volume-and-conservative_interpolate-rows)): the greedy order collapses onto one survivor until its star holds hundreds of tets, and every attempt on it walks the whole star (24,576 tets take 110 s, the 162,000 of tier M over 17 minutes). Bounding the star changes which collapses happen, so it needs a decision on the operation's output first: a valence cap, or a priority that penalises a growing star, measured for quality against today's output. **M**
- **5.2 The wheels' parallel runtimes.** The Linux wheels build the THREADS backend since v19.1 ([installation](./installation.md)), a `std::thread` pool in the core that needs no runtime library. What is left: one manylinux wheel built in CI, measured against the last SEQ wheel on `bench.py --sizes L --formats vtu,xdmf` and `tools/bench_ops.sh`, its numbers recorded in [benchmarks](./benchmarks.md#a-threads-backend-for-the-linux-wheels); and whether delocate bundles Homebrew's libomp into the macOS wheel (`delocate-listdeps`), which [installation](./installation.md) leaves open. **S**
- **5.5 Operation hot spots.** **M**
  - **5.5.2 `optimize_volume`'s worst-cell smoothing.** The [profile](./benchmarks.md#profiling-optimize_volume) at tier S puts 73% of the row's instructions in `optvol_smooth_worst`, which evaluates every tet of a vertex's star (six cross products and a square root each, `optvol_min_dihedral`) at every line-search probe and every central difference, for the least angle; the comparison sorts of the face and edge tables are 7.4% together, the passes that use them (edge removal, multi-face removal, 2-3 flips) 21%. The `acos` that was 31% of the row is now skipped where it cannot give the least angle. What is left is algorithmic: per probe, evaluate only the tets whose angles lie in the active band, with an argument that the others cannot become the least within the step, inside the determinism pins of [optimize_volume](./optimize_volume.md).
- **5.8 HDF5.** The appended rows of a VTKHDF time series are compressed in parallel since 5.8.1 ([benchmarks](./benchmarks.md#appended-rows-deflated-in-parallel)). **M–L**
  - **5.8.2 The library lock around library calls only.** The process-wide lock ([threads and the GIL](./architecture.md#threads-and-the-gil)) is taken as the first statement of whole readers and writers (`formats/vtkhdf.cpp`, `formats/xdmf.cpp`), so decoding, the parallel deflate and the mesh assembly are serialised across threads too, and the thread-pool route the architecture page recommends gains nothing on the HDF5-family formats. Read the raw data, release the lock, then assemble; compress first, then take the lock to write. The lock's invariant, no two threads inside the libraries, is unchanged.
- **5.9 WASM: bulk copies of JS arrays.** The bindings convert JS arrays with `emscripten::vecFromJSArray` (34 calls in `bindings/wasm/js_bindings.cpp`), element by element through embind, and copy the result again into the `NDArray`. A `typed_memory_view` over the destination, filled by the typed array's `set` (or `convertJSArrayToNumberVector`), writes straight into it. Verify first against emsdk 6.0.3's `val.h`. Probe: a Node script that writes a 1M-tetrahedron JS mesh with `writeMesh`. **S**
- **5.11 Sequences.** The [sequence driver](./sequences.md) keeps at most one mesh alive, by design. An opt-in read-ahead (`Prefetch: 1`, reading step k+1 while step k computes, with the cost of two meshes stated); fields-only reads for the later steps of the formats with an in-file timeline (XDMF, Exodus, VTKHDF, d3plot), which re-read the geometry at every step today; and no second read of each step in the PhysicsNeMo pairing. **M**
- **5.12 Small items.** **S each**
  - **5.12.3 Link-time optimisation.** No target sets `INTERPROCEDURAL_OPTIMIZATION`; a measured trial on the shared core and the native CLI, through the landing gates, with the wheel and binary sizes beside it.

## 6. ParaView plugin {#paraview-plugin}

*Admission: bringing what the library does into ParaView, where much of its output is post-processed. An item mirrors a library feature through the public Python API and never re-implements one, and an operation ParaView already has natively is not wrapped. Audited in November 2026 against `tools/paraview-meshioplusplus-plugin.py`, a reader and a writer of unstructured grids in about 200 lines ([ParaView plugin](./paraview_plugin.md)). The [Blender add-on](./blender.md) is the pattern: its import toggles, export format choice, pure payload layer and bundled zips are mirrored below. Its two defects are [§1](#correctness-debts).*

- **6.1 A pure payload layer.** Move the conversion out of the plugin into `meshioplusplus._paraview`, built on `_interop._to_vtk_payload` (vectorised, wedges permuted, every `polygon*` mapped, points of any width padded) and `_vtk_common.vtk_cells_from_data` (cells split into runs in their original order, the inverse wedge permutation applied), and test it in the default CI matrix without ParaView, as `_blender.py`'s payload is ([architecture](./blender.md#architecture-the-pure-payload-layer)); the plugin keeps only the VTK calls. It closes 1.1 and 1.2, removes the per-cell Python loops of both directions (about 2 s and 150 MB of lists for a million tetrahedra, estimated), keeps the cell order across a write (a limitation the plugin page lists today), and skips string and other non-numeric arrays with a warning where `np.asarray` returns `None` for them now (verify first on ParaView's Exodus output, which carries QA records). Probe: the payload tests fail on today's wedge and `polygonN` handling. **S**
- **6.2 Polyhedra, both ways.** The reader skips polyhedron blocks (the legacy cell layout it builds has no face stream), and so does `_to_vtk_payload`, which drops them for `to_pyvista` too. Build the face stream from the CSR rows the [polyhedron kernel](./polyhedra.md) already holds, and read it back in the writer; fixing the shared payload fixes `to_pyvista` with it. VTK's polyhedron API changed in 9.4, so gate per version and test per ParaView release. Probe: `tests/python/meshes/vtu/two_pieces_polyhedron.vtu` has the same cell count through the plugin as through `meshioplusplus.read`. **M**
- **6.3 Higher-order cells.**
  - **6.3.1 Lagrange and Bézier degrees.** Pass `HigherOrderDegrees` for the arbitrary-order VTK Lagrange and Bézier cells and the rational weights of the Bézier ones ([cell types](./cell_types.md)), which the plugin drops today. **S**
  - **6.3.2 The fixed-order high cells.** `triangle10` to `hexahedron64` have no VTK id in `_vtk_common`, so the reader skips them; map them to `VTK_LAGRANGE_*` through the lattice the [node-ordering registry](./node_ordering.md) holds. Verify first whether the VTU writer has the same gap, which would make this a library item the plugin inherits. Probe: a Gmsh P3 triangle mesh shows a non-zero cell count. **M**
- **6.4 Time.** ParaView sees one step of every file: the reader has no `RequestInformation`, and it is registered with `support_reload=False`.
  - **6.4.1 Time steps in the reader.** Publish `TIME_STEPS` and `TIME_RANGE` from `read_metadata(path)["time_values"]` ([sequences](./sequences.md) lists the formats that report steps), map `UPDATE_TIME_STEP` to `read(time_step=i)`, open file series (`out_*.vtu`) through `TimeSeries`, and support reload. Cache the metadata per file identity, since some formats answer it with a full read. Probe: a three-step `frd` written by `write_sequence` (no HDF5 needed, so it runs in today's CI job) gives `TimestepValues == [0, 0.5, 2]`, and `UpdatePipeline(2.0)` returns the last step's field. **M**
  - **6.4.2 Every step in the writer.** Iterate the input's time steps into `write_sequence` for the series formats, and fan out to `{step}` files otherwise. Probe: a three-step input written as `frd` reports three `time_values`. **M**
- **6.5 Read options and status.** Point and cell array checkboxes filled from `read_metadata` and passed as `arrays=`, geometry only (`points_only`), `piece` and `ghosts` ([selective reads](./selective_read.md)), as the Blender import toggles do; the STEP and IGES tessellation (`deflection`, `angular_deflection`, [STEP](./formats/step.md#reading)); and which add-ons are loaded (`has_occt`, `has_netgen`, `has_mmg`), so a failed STEP read explains itself. Probe: an unticked array is absent from the fetched dataset. **S**
- **6.6 Named regions.** The reader drops `regions`, `cell_sets` and side sets altogether.
  - **6.6.1 As arrays.** Carry them as the VTU convention's field arrays or as `region:` masks, which `_region_field_data` and `_interop` already write and read back, so they survive a round trip through ParaView. **S**
  - **6.6.2 As an assembly.** Return a `vtkPartitionedDataSetCollection` with a `vtkDataAssembly`, as ParaView's own Exodus (IOSS) reader does, built from `split(by="regions")` with side regions through `facet_nodes`, so a region is a block the user can tick. Probe: a Gmsh fixture with two physical groups shows both names in the assembly, each block with its region's cell count. **M–L**
- **6.7 The writer's inputs and options.** Accept any `vtkDataSet` and composite inputs (`composite_data_supported=False` today): merge the leaves with `merge`, as the Blender export does, and turn block names back into cell regions. Add a format choice; the encoding, codec and float format through `mcp._tools._variant_kwargs`, the single owner of each format's spelling, which the pipeline's `Output` uses too; and a [provenance](./provenance.md) record naming ParaView as the source. Probe: ParaView's `Sphere()` source writes to `.stl`, a two-block multiblock writes `.msh` with two physical groups, and `Codec=lz4` puts `vtkLZ4DataCompressor` in a VTU header. **M**
- **6.8 A generic *meshio++ Pipeline* filter.** One filter that takes the [settings pipeline](./pipeline.md)'s `Operations` list as JSON and applies it to its input, so every operation the pipeline has is reachable at once; it builds on [`apply_operations`](./pipeline.md#in-memory-apply-operations), which applies the steps to a mesh in memory with `run_pipeline`'s validation and report. The model steps (`MeshCadSurface`, `FitCad`, `ProjectToCad`, the IGA steps) stay reachable only through it, inside one filter, since a B-rep cannot travel through VTK. Probe: `[{"Op": "Refine"}]` on tetrahedra gives eight times the cells, and an unknown op raises naming it. **M**
- **6.9 Dedicated filters where ParaView has no equivalent.** After 6.8 each is a thin proxy with typed properties: `remesh`, `remesh_volume`, `tetrahedralize`, `octree_mesh`, `boundary_layer`, `optimize_volume`, `decimate_volume`, `refine` with its selectors and `undo_green`, `agglomerate`, `tet_to_poly` and `poly_to_tet`, the adaptation chain (`hessian`, `estimate_error`, `error_size`, then `adapt` with the MMG add-on), `conservative_interpolate` and `shrinkwrap` (two inputs), `repair`, `find_intersections`, `thickness`, `check_conformity`, `select`, the interface tools (`region_adjacency`, `find_interface`, the contact pairs), periodic node matching, `partition` with ghost layers and `reorder`. ## 7. Scan metrology and reverse engineering {#scan-metrology}

*Admission: an operation a scan-inspection or scan-to-CAD workflow needs — measuring a 3-D scan against its intended geometry, then turning it into a CAD body — that the library lacks, checked against the code before it was listed (November 2026). The list came from reviewing the published feature descriptions of [ScanRuler](https://github.com/CNCKitchen/ScanRuler) (its README, `docs/manual.md`, `plugins/reverse/README.md` and `plugins/mesh-tools/README.md`), a browser tool for exactly this workflow. ScanRuler is AGPL-3.0: only ideas are taken, its source was not read for this list and none of it may be copied, ported or paraphrased into this MIT repository ([non-goals](#non-goals-and-decisions-taken)); every algorithm below is named from the open literature or from what meshio++ already has. The defaults quoted (3σ, 60°, 99.9%, ...) are the published behaviour of a tool users compare against, a starting point to measure, not a specification. Each item names its consumer, the kernels it reuses, its probe and its effort; an operation lands on every surface and in the MCP server, as the change checklist requires, unless the item says otherwise.*

What ships already, so nothing here re-proposes it: [`fit_primitives`](./fit_primitives.md) (least-squares plane, sphere, cylinder, cone and torus per patch, RMS and max residual), [`segment`](./segment.md)'s primitive method, [`select`](./select.md)'s seed-grown patches, [`fit_cad`](./fit_cad.md) (analytic, blend, thread, freeform and patch-network faces, `constrain_primitives`, a Hausdorff report), [`distance_to_surface`](./sdf.md) (signed by the angle-weighted pseudonormal), [`hausdorff_distance`](./hausdorff.md), [`register_rigid`](./registration.md) (local ICP), [`thickness`](./thickness.md) (one ray per facet), [`slice`](./slice.md), [`crop`](./crop.md), [`repair`](./repair.md), [`smooth`](./smooth.md), [`decimate`](./decimate.md), [`mirror`](./mirror.md), [`shrinkwrap`](./shrinkwrap.md), the `compute_sdf` + `isosurface` offset recipe, the colormaps and the [length units](./formats.md#length-units).

- **7.1 Measurement-grade primitive fits.** `fit_primitives` answers "which primitive is this patch, and how well does it fit"; an inspection needs "fit this feature I point at, robustly, and report the numbers a metrology report carries". All items extend `operations/fit_primitives.cpp` and its kernels (`detail/lsq_fit`, `detail/primitive_geom`), keep the fit deterministic (no random sampling, ties to the lowest index) and add only new options, field-data columns and report keys (pure additions: an [ABI review](./abi_reviews.md), not a bump; the MCP tool's report fields and `tests/python/test_mcp.py` move with them).
  - **7.1.1 Fit a feature from a seed or a region.** Today a selection reaches `fit_primitives` only as an integer cell-data label (`patch_data`), or the whole mesh with `patch_data=""`. Add `region="name"` (a cell region, as `select` writes) and `seed=(x, y, z)` with `type="cylinder"` (or `"auto"`): from the facet nearest the seed (`select`'s seed lookup), grow across smooth edges while the refitted primitive keeps every corner within the tolerance (the growth `segment(method="primitive")` already does from its own seeds, factored out so both call one function), then peel the rim: drop boundary rings whose normals turn away from the fitted surface's by more than an angle, so a fillet or a flared bore mouth does not bend the fit. Several seeds fit one primitive across disconnected patches (a bore interrupted by a cross hole). Output: the fitted cells as a named region plus the usual `fit:` rows. Consumer: measuring one bore, boss or face of a scan without segmenting the whole part. Probe: a seed on the filleted bore of a tessellated bracket returns the bore's cells only (no fillet cell) and its radius to 1e-9; two seeds on a bore split by a cross hole give one cylinder. **M**
  - **7.1.2 Robust estimation and sigma trimming.** Acceptance today is RMS plus `min_inlier_fraction`, and every point weighs the same, so a scan's stray points, a burr or a fixture drag the fit. Add a deterministic consensus start (least median of squares, Rousseeuw 1984, over a fixed ordered schedule of minimal subsets drawn by stride from the Hilbert-sorted points — no RNG, so the result is byte-identical across backends and thread counts) and `used_points="all" | "3sigma" | "2sigma" | "1sigma"` (default `"3sigma"` for the seed and region modes, `"all"` for the patch mode so today's output does not move): refit, drop points beyond k·σ, refit until the used set is stable. Report `sigma` (the RMS orthogonal distance of the used points), `num_used`, `num_rejected`. Probe: 5% gross outliers 10σ off a noisy cylinder move the recovered radius by less than one σ with `"3sigma"`, and by more without it. **M**
  - **7.1.3 Form error and extremes.** Add per patch, as `fit:patch_*` columns and report keys: the peak-to-peak form error (largest positive minus largest negative signed orthogonal distance of the used points: flatness, cylindricity, sphericity, circularity under the least-squares reference), the minimum and maximum diameters the surface reaches beside the least-squares one for spheres, cylinders and circles (twice the smallest and largest radial distance), a cylinder's or cone's axial length (extent of the points projected on the axis), its arc span in degrees (largest angular gap subtracted from 360), and a plane's extent (the two in-plane PCA extents). Consumer: every inspection report; it is also what 7.2.3 reads. Probe: a tessellated half cylinder reports an arc of 180 degrees and a form error of 0 to rounding; a cylinder of two radii (a step) reports Ø min and Ø max equal to them. **S**
  - **7.1.4 Minimum-zone and envelope fits.** `criterion="least_squares" | "minimum_zone" | "min_circumscribed" | "max_inscribed"`: the Chebyshev reference that GD&T form tolerances are defined against (the narrowest zone holding every point, ISO 1101), and the envelope references of a shaft and a hole (the smallest enclosing and the largest inscribed cylinder or circle). Start from the least-squares fit and solve by iterated linearisation into a linear programme (the minimum-zone plane is a linear programme in its own right; the circle, cylinder and sphere are solved as a sequence of them), with the exact LP solved by a small dense simplex the core owns — no solver dependency; verify first the algorithms of the literature (Carr and Ferreira 1995 for the minimum-zone formulations; Shunmugam 1986 for minimum-zone circles and spheres) and the definitions in ISO 12780 (straightness), 12781 (flatness), 12181 (roundness) and 12180 (cylindricity). The least-squares result stays the default. Consumer: a form tolerance that matches a CMM's, which least squares overestimates; ScanRuler lists these fits as its own future work. Probe: the minimum-zone flatness of a point set drawn between two parallel planes 0.1 apart, with points on both, is 0.1 to 1e-12, while least squares reports more. **L**
- **7.2 Elements, dimensions and GD&T.** Nothing in the library turns fitted primitives into dimensions or tolerance checks; `constrain_primitives` detects coaxial and perpendicular groups but snaps them rather than reporting their deviation. **L in total, design pass first**
  - **7.2.1 Design pass: the element table.** Measured elements (each fitted row of 7.1, with its used points) and constructed ones (the line where two planes meet, an axis, a sphere's or circle's centre, the mid-plane of two planes, a point projected onto a plane, a plane through three points), each with a stable id and a name, serialisable as JSON. Decide where it lives: field-data rows on the mesh (survive a write to VTU, lost by most formats) or a side channel beside it as `NurbsInfo` and `BrepInfo` are ([non-goals](#non-goals-and-decisions-taken)), and how a pipeline step refers to an element (by name, as regions are). Decide too whether a constructed element records the formula (so it updates when an input refits) or only its value.
  - **7.2.2 Dimensions.** Distance (point–point, point–plane, point–line, line–line, plane–plane for parallel planes, centre distance) and angle (line–line, line–plane, plane–plane) between elements, each with an optional nominal and ± tolerance and a PASS/FAIL. Consumer: a first-article check of a printed or machined part against its drawing.
  - **7.2.3 Tolerances.** Form: flatness, cylindricity, sphericity, circularity (from 7.1.3, under the reference 7.1.4 selects). Orientation: parallelism, perpendicularity and angularity at a typed basic angle, each the width of the zone, oriented by the datum, that holds the toleranced feature's used points (for a plane to a datum plane: the spread of the points' distances along the datum normal, for perpendicularity the spread along the datum direction rotated by 90 degrees). Location: coaxiality and concentricity, the diameter of the zone about the datum axis holding the toleranced axis' ends. Position and runout, and datum reference frames with more than one datum, as follow-ups after this design is used. Each with an optional limit and PASS/FAIL. Cite ISO 1101 / ASME Y14.5 for the definitions and verify first each zone's construction against them.
  - **7.2.4 Surfaces.** `measure(mesh, spec)` in Python, where `spec` (a dict or a JSON file) lists elements to fit (7.1.1's seeds or regions), constructions, dimensions and tolerances, returning the element table and a report; `meshioplusplus measure scan.stl spec.json` in both CLIs (text table, `--json`); a `Measure` pipeline step; an MCP tool. A native implementation in `operations/measure.cpp` so C, WASM and the native CLI share it. Probe: a tessellated block whose side is tilted by 0.05 degrees reports a perpendicularity of `height * tan(0.05°)` to 1e-9, and a spec with a 0.01 limit reports FAIL.
- **7.3 Alignment beyond local ICP.** `register_rigid` finds the nearest local optimum and needs a start within a few degrees ([registration](./registration.md#what-it-will-not-do)); a scanner's frame is arbitrary. All items reuse `operations/registration.cpp` (the Horn closed form, the point-to-plane step) and the closest-point query of `sdf`.
  - **7.3.1 A global start.** `register_rigid(..., initial="auto")`: align the two principal-axis frames (area-weighted covariance of the surfaces, or of the points for a cloud), try the 24 proper rotations that map one frame's signed axes onto the other's (the PCA axes' signs and order are ambiguous for near-equal eigenvalues), run point-to-plane ICP from each at a coarse tolerance on a subsample, and refine the one with the lowest trimmed RMS; ties go to the lowest candidate index. Report each candidate's RMS and the chosen index. Optional `symmetric_check` warns when the best two candidates are within a tolerance of each other (a symmetric part). Consumer: scan-to-nominal deviation (7.4) without a manual pre-alignment. Probe: a copy of a non-symmetric bracket rotated by 150 degrees about a skew axis registers back to 1e-9, which today's identity start does not. **M**
  - **7.3.2 Landmark alignment.** `register_landmarks(source_points, target_points, weights=None)`: the weighted Horn solve on three or more picked pairs, returning the matrix and the per-pair residuals; the start the viewer (7.9.1) or a script gives `register_rigid`. Probe: three exact pairs recover a known transform to 1e-12, and two pairs are refused by name. **S**
  - **7.3.3 Region-restricted and adaptive fine fit.** `source_region` and `target_region` restrict the pairs to the part's true surface, excluding fixtures, supports and turntables; `max_distance="auto"` rejects pairs beyond a multiple of the median pair distance at each iteration (adaptive trimming, as in Chetverikov et al. 2002's trimmed ICP), reported as `num_rejected`. Probe: a scan with a fixture plate the nominal lacks registers within 1e-6 with the part's region named, and is pulled off by the plate without it. **S**
  - **7.3.4 3-2-1 datum alignment.** `align_datums(mesh, primary, secondary, origin=None)`: the primary element (a plane, or a cylinder's axis) sets Z, the secondary (a plane or an axis, projected) sets X, an optional point, centre or the intersection of the three sets the origin; returns the 4×4 matrix `transform` applies and moves the element table with it. After 7.2.1. Probe: a block fitted on three faces is aligned so those faces lie on the coordinate planes to 1e-12. **S**
  - **7.3.5 Auto-align from the scan alone.** Build an area-weighted histogram of facet normals on the Gauss sphere (a fixed subdivided-icosahedron binning), extract the dominant direction and the dominant direction orthogonal to it, and propose a pose that lays the part on its largest flat face (or, for a part whose dominant feature is a cylinder, on the end of its axis); an opening in an open scan is told from a face by the sign of the boundary loop's vector area. Report the share of the surface whose normals agree with the proposed axes, so the user can reject it. Consumer: a consistent frame for parts without a nominal. Probe: a scanned block rotated arbitrarily comes back axis-aligned, with an agreeing share above 0.9. **M**
  - **7.3.6 Symmetry plane.** `find_symmetry_plane(mesh)`: from each of the three principal planes (and an optional seed plane), mirror the surface (`mirror`), register the mirror image to the original with point-to-plane ICP, and recover the symmetry plane from the product of the reflection and the rigid motion (a reflection whose plane is the bisector); keep the candidate of lowest RMS, coarse on a subsample then fine. Report the plane, `rms` and the share of samples within tolerance, and refuse to report a plane below a stated share. Then `mirror(plane="auto")` and `align_datums` can use it. Consumer: symmetric parts, and the mirror step of reverse engineering (model half, mirror the rest). Probe: a bracket symmetric about a tilted plane, with noise, finds the plane within 1e-4 radians and 1e-4 of the diagonal. **M**
- **7.4 Deviation analysis.** `distance_to_surface` attaches a signed distance per point; what an inspection reads from it — a histogram, the share within tolerance, where the material is missing — the user computes by hand.
  - **7.4.1 A deviation report.** `deviation(scan, reference, tolerance=None, ...)` wraps `distance_to_surface` and returns the scan with `deviation:distance` plus a report: min, max, mean, RMS, σ, the percentiles 1, 5, 50, 95 and 99, a histogram (fixed bins over a range), the share within ±`tolerance`, and a suggested colour range (the rounded 95th percentile of |d|). The far-side rule: `max_normal_angle` (default 90 degrees) skips nearest triangles whose normal turns away from the query's by more than that, so a thin wall is not measured to its back face; this needs a filter inside the nearest-triangle search (`detail/surface_distance.cpp`), not after it, since the filtered answer is a different triangle — verify first that the filter keeps the (distance², triangle id) tiebreak and its cost on the tier rows. With `register=True` it runs 7.3.1 and 7.3.3 first and reports the matrix. Consumer: scan-to-nominal inspection; a surrogate's reconstructed geometry against its ground truth. Probe: a sphere scan of radius r + 0.01 against a sphere of radius r reports mean 0.01 and a share of 1.0 within ±0.02; a 1 mm plate offset by 0.05 reads 0.05 on both faces with the rule and up to 1.05 without it. **M**
  - **7.4.2 Against a fitted element.** `deviation(scan, element)` where the reference is a fitted primitive (a `fit:patch_params` row, or an 7.2.1 element): the signed analytic distance from `detail/primitive_geom`, no reference file and no alignment. Consumer: flatness maps of a face, the bow of a printed plate. Probe: points on a cylinder of radius 2 offset by 0.1 read 0.1 against the fitted cylinder of radius 2. **S**
  - **7.4.3 Volume errors.** `deviation_regions(scan, tolerance)`: connected components (across shared edges) of cells beyond +tolerance (excess material) and beyond −tolerance (missing material), each with its area, extreme and mean deviation, signed volume estimate (∫ d dA over the region), centroid and bounding box, sorted by volume, and written as named cell regions so `split` or `crop` can isolate them. Reuse `select`'s threshold criterion and the component pass of `split(by="component")`. Probe: a box with a 2 mm bump and a 1 mm dent lists exactly two regions with volumes matching the features to the tessellation's error. **S–M**
  - **7.4.4 Deviation exports.** Bake the map into vertex colours through `detail/colormap` (continuous or banded at fixed steps) for PLY and glTF; and `exaggerate(scan, field, scale)`: points displaced along their normals by `scale × d`, with d clamped at the colour range and smoothed over about 1% of the diagonal so noise does not become fur — a mesh for an animation or a figure, the measured scan left untouched. Probe: the exaggerated sphere of 7.4.1 has radius `r + scale × 0.01`. **S**
- **7.5 Wall thickness beyond one ray.** `thickness` casts one ray per facet along its normal ([thickness](./thickness.md#the-ray)): exact between parallel walls, too long by 1/cos θ where the walls are not, and it needs a consistently oriented surface, which a scan often is not.
  - **7.5.1 The sphere and cone methods.** `method="ray" | "sphere" | "cone"`. `sphere`: from the ray's midpoint, the distance to the nearest surface point other than the facet's own neighbourhood, doubled — one extra closest-point query per facet through `detail/tri_bvh`, never longer than the ray and much closer to the true wall where the faces diverge (the maximal-inscribed-sphere definition collapses at sharp edges, so it is not offered). `cone`: a fixed deterministic fan of rays within `cone_angle` of the normal, the shortest hit, which finds a chamfer's or a tapered rib's narrow spot. Probe: a wedge whose faces meet at 30 degrees reads, at a given point, a sphere value within 2% of the analytic inscribed distance and a ray value 1/cos θ longer. **M**
  - **7.5.2 Scan-safe hits.** `two_sided=True` counts both faces of every triangle as an exit (scan winding is unreliable); `max_normal_deviation` (default 60 degrees) steps over surfaces more edge-on to the ray than that, so a cone does not escape through a convex edge; a facet without an exit within `max_distance` (default a fifth of the diagonal) is NaN, not zero; the report adds the 2nd and 95th percentiles a colour ramp is set from. Probe: a closed box with randomly flipped facets reads its wall with `two_sided=True` and misreads it without. **S**
- **7.6 Sections to sketches.** `slice` returns a section as unordered line segments; a CAD sketch, a drawing or a CAM path needs ordered chains of lines and arcs.
  - **7.6.1 Chained sections.** `slice(..., chain=True)`: segments joined end to end into ordered open and closed polylines (`line` cells in chain order, with `section:chain` and `section:closed` cell data), chains shorter than `min_length` dropped; `sections(mesh, direction, offsets)` for a series of parallel cuts. The marching cutter already welds its endpoints, so the chaining is a walk over point degree; a vertex of degree other than two splits a chain. Probe: a slice of a torus gives exactly two closed chains, in a deterministic order and orientation. **S**
  - **7.6.2 Lines, arcs and circles from a chain.** `fit_curves(sections, tolerance)`: split each chain into runs a line or a circular arc explains within the tolerance (breakpoints where the discrete curvature jumps, then a merge-and-split pass, then a robust refit with 7.1.2's trimming), join the corners of consecutive pieces at their intersection, optionally snap lines within an angle to the sketch's axes or to 45 degrees, and report each piece's residual; a whole closed chain within tolerance of a circle is one circle. Output: a sketch entity list (type, endpoints, centre, radius, sweep, residual; JSON-serialisable) in the plane's 2-D frame, plus the line mesh annotated with each segment's piece. Reuse `detail/lsq_fit`'s circle fit (today internal to `fit_cad`'s cylinder unroll). Consumer: 7.6.4, 7.6.5 and 7.7.4; drawings of a scanned profile. Probe: a section of a tessellated plate with a slot gives four lines and two arcs, radii to 1e-6. **M**
  - **7.6.3 Silhouette outlines.** `silhouette(mesh, normal)`: the outer boundary of the surface projected onto a plane, with holes, as chains (then 7.6.2 for lines and arcs); `clearance` offsets it (positive for a loose fit, negative for a press fit), corners turning less than an angle are rounded with arcs, and `extrude=True` makes a prism from the part's lowest to its highest point along the normal (a nest, a jig or a foam insert). It needs a robust union of many 2-D triangles: verify first whether one built on the core's Shewchuk predicates (`detail/predicates`) and the planar CDT (`detail/cdt2d`) is small enough to own, against Clipper2 (BSL-1.0) as a vendored dependency. Probe: the silhouette of a sphere along any axis is one circle of its radius within the tessellation's sag; a bracket's has its holes. **M–L**
  - **7.6.4 DXF.** Reopened from format reach's *Considered, not queued*: a sketch (7.6.2) or a silhouette (7.6.3) crosses to CAD and CAM at 1:1 as DXF, the format every CAD sketcher, laser cutter and CNC post-processor imports. A `dxf` format whose writer emits `LINE`, `ARC`, `CIRCLE`, `LWPOLYLINE` and `SPLINE` entities (ASCII DXF R12 or 2000, verify first which the common importers need for `SPLINE`), in millimetres with `$INSUNITS` from `meshio:length_unit`, writing a line mesh as `LINE`s when it carries no sketch; and, since every registered format reads and writes ([non-goals](#non-goals-and-decisions-taken)), a reader limited to the entities the writer emits, refusing anything else by name (the `svg` and `tikz` rule). Python reference, native twin, `SPEC` declaration in `tests/python/conformance_spec.py`, a `doc/formats/dxf.md` page. Probe: a written sketch opens in ezdxf (MIT, a test-only dependency) with the same entities, and reads back to the same line mesh. **M**
  - **7.6.5 STEP curves and element solids.** Write sketches and section chains to STEP as curves in a named `GEOMETRIC_SET` (lines, circles, B-spline curves), and fitted elements as geometry a CAD user can snap to: a plane as a bounded face, a cylinder as a solid with flat lids over its measured length, a sphere as a ball, a silhouette prism as a solid with planar and cylindrical sides — through the existing STEP writer and `detail/step_brep`, no CAD kernel needed. Probe: the STEP file of a fitted cylinder imports into OpenCASCADE as one valid solid of the measured radius and length. **S–M**
- **7.7 Reverse engineering: closing the loop.** `fit_cad` turns a skin into a solid in one pass and reports its Hausdorff deviation; what is missing is how much of each face the scan actually supports, a target to stop at and a driver that iterates towards it.
  - **7.7.1 Support per face.** For each face of the solid, the share of its area within tolerance of a scan sample (sample the face as the report's `face_deviation` does, query the scan's distance), classed `measured` (most of it), `partial` and `inferred` (no scan near: hidden interiors, the far side of a through-hole, faces closed by the solid's construction); per-face field data and report counts. Consumer: an honest report of what in a reverse-engineered body was measured and what was guessed. Probe: a scan of a box with its bottom missing yields a solid whose bottom face is `inferred`, the other five `measured`. **S**
  - **7.7.2 Targets and an iterating driver.** `fit_cad(..., target_within=0.9)` reports the share of scan samples within ±tolerance of the solid and whether it meets the target; `part_class="analytic" | "cast" | "freeform"` sets defaults for the target (0.90, 0.85, 0.75) and for the tolerances; and `fit_cad_iterate(scan, ...)` re-segments the faces of the worst deviation (`refine_patches`, a finer `segment` tolerance on their cells), refits and stops at the target, at no improvement or after a set number of rounds, reporting each round. Deterministic: the same input and options give the same rounds. Probe: on a skin whose first pass demotes a cylinder to facets, the driver recovers it in a later round and the share within tolerance rises monotonically. **M**
  - **7.7.3 Explicit constraint control.** `constrain_primitives` decides its coaxial, parallel, perpendicular and equal-radius groups by its own tolerances ([constrained primitives](./fit_cad.md#constrained-primitives)). Add a force and a forbid list (pairs of patch ids and a relation), angle and distance tolerances as options (snap within 0.5 degrees, merge coplanar planes and coaxial axes within a distance, as a scan wants), and a report of every relation applied with its residual. Verify first what the pass exposes today; the parked limits (parallel but offset axes, angles other than right ones, patterns) stay parked. Probe: forbidding the perpendicularity of two faces leaves their fitted normals unchanged, and the report lists every relation the default run applies. **S**
  - **7.7.4 Profile solids through the CAD provider.** Extrude a closed 7.6.2 sketch along its plane's normal (to a distance, or to the scan's extent), revolve one about a fitted axis, and fillet, chamfer or shell the result, as new `CadKernelProvider` methods (`providers.hpp`, OpenCASCADE behind them in `src/cpp/providers/occt/`), so a turned or prismatic part becomes a clean solid from a few sections, compared to the scan with 7.4.1. **Design pass first**: decide the smallest scripted API (a list of feature dicts in a pipeline step) and where it stops — the interactive parametric modeller is out ([non-goals](#non-goals-and-decisions-taken)). Probe: two sections of a scanned turned shaft revolved about its fitted axis give a solid within 0.05 of the scan on 95% of its samples. **L**
- **7.8 Scan editing.** The operations a scan needs before it is measured or fitted that [`repair`](./repair.md), [`clean`](./clean.md), [`crop`](./crop.md) and [`smooth`](./smooth.md) do not cover.
  - **7.8.1 `remove_islands`.** Drop connected components (by shared edges, or by shared points) whose bounding-box diagonal, area or cell count is below a threshold (`relative=True`: a fraction of the whole mesh's diagonal, default 1/100), reporting what was dropped; a dry run returns the labels only. Reuse `split(by="component")`'s labelling. Probe: a sphere plus ten small scan crumbs keeps the sphere only. **S**
  - **7.8.2 `flip_normals`.** Reverse the winding of all cells or of a region (`region=`), moving `Side` region entries as `mirror` does, with no point moved; `repair` orients consistently and outward, but a user who wants the opposite orientation, or a part of the surface flipped, has no operation. Probe: flipping twice is bit-identical to the input. **S**
  - **7.8.3 Cut and cap.** `cut(mesh, origin, normal, keep="below", cap=True)`: the exact cut of `slice` with the kept side's cells split at the plane (as `crop` keeps whole cells, it does not do this today), and the cut's loops triangulated by the planar CDT (`detail/cdt2d`, the triangulation `triangulate_polygon_2d` exposes) so a closed part stays closed. Probe: a closed sphere cut at its equator gives a closed hemisphere whose volume is half the sphere's to the tessellation's error. **S–M**
  - **7.8.4 Offset, thicken and hollow.** `offset_surface(mesh, distance)`: move each vertex along its normal so that both faces of a sharp edge keep the distance (the vertex moves by distance over the cosine of the half-angle, bounded), with the `compute_sdf` + `isosurface` recipe as the reference it is checked against; `thicken(surface, thickness, side="inward" | "outward" | "both")`: an open surface into a closed shell (the offset plus its rim joined by quads); `hollow(solid, wall)`: a closed surface with an inner, reversed offset wall. Reuse `boundary_layer`'s normal directions and its no-fold guard. Probe: a thickened hemisphere is closed and `thickness` reads the requested wall to 1%. **M**
  - **7.8.5 Deviation-bounded decimation.** `decimate`'s `max_error` bounds a quadric error in squared units, not a distance a user can state. Add `max_deviation` (the largest distance the result may lie from the input, checked two-sided by `hausdorff_distance` on the result, and held for a `coverage` share of samples, default 0.999) and `max_faces`, so "the lightest mesh within 0.05 mm, at most 50,000 faces" is one call. Within the collapse loop, a per-vertex bound on the distance moved (the accumulated quadric's distance bound, verify first) stops a collapse early; the final Hausdorff check is the guarantee. Probe: a scan decimated with `max_deviation=0.05` has a measured two-sided Hausdorff distance at most 0.05 on the requested share. **M**
  - **7.8.6 Faired hole filling.** Verify first what `repair`'s fill does after it triangulates a hole (minimum-area or minimum-dihedral triangulation, Liepa 2003's refinement and fairing). If it stops at the triangulation: refine the patch to the rim's edge density and fair it, `fill_style="flat" | "curved"` (minimising membrane or thin-plate energy, the Laplacian of `smooth` with the rim fixed). Probe: a hole cut in a sphere is filled with a patch whose points lie within 1% of the radius with `"curved"`. **M**
  - **7.8.7 Booleans with fitted solids.** Subtracting a measured cylinder from a scan, or adding a modelled body to it, waits on the surface-boolean spike (7.8).
- **7.9 Viewer and agents.**
  - **7.9.1 A measuring mode in the browser viewer.** The viewer picks cells and measures the distance between two clicked points ([viewer](./viewer.md)). Add: click a feature to grow and fit it (7.1.1 through the WASM bindings), show its fit values and σ; angle and radius dimensions between fitted elements (7.2.2); a deviation view with its histogram and a tolerance count (7.4.1), and a thickness view (7.5). Each lands after the library item it calls. Probe: a viewer test (`src/viewer/`) clicks a bore of a fixture and reads its radius in the panel. **M**
  - **7.9.2 Workflow prompts in the MCP server.** The server registers tools and two resources (`meshioplusplus://formats`, `meshioplusplus://version`) and no prompts. Add MCP prompts (or guide resources) for "measure this scan" and "reverse-engineer this scan": the order of the tools (repair, align, segment, fit, measure or `fit_cad`, deviation, iterate), the defaults per part class of 7.7.2 and the report fields to check, so an agent follows the documented workflow rather than inventing one. Text only, kept in step with the tools by a test that every tool a prompt names exists. Probe: `prompts/list` returns them. **S**

Considered for this section, not queued: measuring 2-D flatbed-scanner images (subpixel edge detection and calibration: images, not meshes); an interactive parametric CAD modeller (a sketch constraint solver, a feature history, thread and insert libraries; see the non-goals); input-device and UI conveniences of a desktop tool; a project archive format (a [pipeline](./pipeline.md) file and [provenance](./provenance.md) already record a run); repair pieces cut to fit a fractured part. Revisit any of them when a consumer asks with a workflow in hand.

## Non-goals and decisions taken

Recorded so they are not re-proposed as gaps.

- **Every registered format reads and writes, in both engines** — decided in October 2026, when the parity tier closed; it reversed the earlier non-goals that kept result files and the export-only formats read-only. A format reads and writes in the C++ core and in its Python twin, so it reaches every surface (C, Fortran, Julia, R, WASM, both CLIs and the MCP server's format capabilities); a core-only path is unreadable from the surfaces that have no fallback, a Python-only one has no twin to check it against. `read(write(m))` equals `m` within the limits the format itself imposes (single precision, a cell type it has no slot for, a concept it cannot express): a loss that is a fact of the format is stated on its page and in the [conformance](./conformance.md) notes, and one that is only an unwritten feature is a gap that reopens a *Reader and writer parity* section here. A new format or direction lands as `AGENTS.md` says (native writer registered in `registry.cpp`, parameters through `WriteOptions`, the Python twin behind `core_declined`, the arrays a reader returns written back under the same names, a `SPEC` declaration and a *Writing* section). The exemptions are recorded in `tests/python/conformance_spec.py` (`READ_ONLY`, `ENGINE_EXEMPT`, `PARTIAL_ENGINE_EXEMPT`) and checked by `test_conformance.py`:
  - **Pure 2-D drawings.** `svg` and `tikz` draw a mesh rather than store it: their readers parse only the structure meshio++'s own writers emit, return the geometry as planar (`z = 0`) and warn once per read that depth, and whatever else the drawing could not hold, is lost; anything else in the file is a `ReadError` naming the construct, never a guess.
  - **`ansys_rst_cyclic`**, which is not a file but the full rotor a cyclic `.rst` expands to; its write side is `ansys_rst`'s cyclic option. **`step` and `iges`** outside a build with OpenCASCADE: reading a CAD model means a CAD kernel, which the `meshioplusplus-occt` add-on wheel (since v18.0.0) and the release binaries carry and a source build finds when it can ([skin to CAD](./cad_fit.md), [STEP](./formats/step.md#reading)); on WASM the lean build is the exempt one, and the [full build](./wasm.md#lean-and-full-builds) carries the kernel.
  - **One engine only, by decision**: GiD (gidpost and its Gauss points are implemented once, in the core), the OpenFOAM case writer and its zone, field and multi-region reading, CGNS's face-based polyhedra (the writer's winding repair is a discrete branch two implementations could resolve differently), the CAD readers, and the `step` writer's merged and fitted faces (`faces="planar"`/`"fitted"`, with `segment`'s primitive method that feeds them: loop walking, vertex snapping and the primitive growth are discrete branches too, so the twin writes `faces="facet"` byte for byte and nothing else); the Python entry points raise naming the missing core rather than guess.
- **What the parity tier left as facts of the format**, each stated on the format's page and in the conformance notes, none an unwritten feature:
  - **`nastran_op2`** writes the MSC dialect only (32-bit little-endian words, SORT1 tables). 64-bit words, big-endian files, NX/Simcenter's interlaced table names and double-precision GRIDs are read and detected, not written: a post-processor reads the MSC dialect with no flags, and whether NX accepts it is a vendor check ([§2.2.14](#awaiting-a-licensed-run)). The random PSD, CRM and ATO tables are written as SORT1 magnitudes, as the reader reads them (MSC writes them SORT2, and pyNastran reads only the RMS and NO ones in SORT1); no SORT2 pivot is written. Elements on scalar points (`CELAS3`/`CELAS4`, `CDAMP3`/`CDAMP4`, `CMASS3`/`CMASS4`) and `CBUSH1D` and `CBEND` have no cell type — `Mesh` has no scalar-point entity to hang them on — and are skipped with a warning.
  - **`nastran_h5`** writes a table only where an MSC sample fixes its layout: `CBUSH`, `CGAP`, `CMASS` and `CONM1` elements, and `MAT5`, `MAT8`, `MAT9`, `PBARL`, `PBEAML` and `PCOMPG` values, have no table in the six MSC Nastran 2020 files at hand, so the elements are left out and the values go on the plain cards, each with one warning; the OP2 file holds them.
  - **`lsdyna_d3plot`** leaves the rigid roads' full rigid-body velocities (`NDIM = 9`) out of the state, and writes an airbag variable under a title-case name, since the names of a real file are in no manual available.
  - **`radioss_anim`** writes the nodal normals as zeros: `anim_to_vtk` ignores them and their 16-bit encoding is undocumented.
  - **`abaqus_fil`** writes 8-byte words only, the reader's width; a single-precision build's 4-byte layout has no description or sample.
  - **`ansys_rst`** has no complex (harmonic) results, read or written: no public sample or open reader covers them, and DPF alone reads them. The nodal coordinate systems are written as `ansys:node_angles` and agree with pymapdl-reader only for single-axis angles, since it applies the three turns in the opposite order.
  - **`xplt`** writes a per-surface-node variable as `FMT_NODE` in surface order, where the reader averages the values a node on two surfaces has into one point value, which both surfaces then write.
  - **`marc` and `marc_t19`**: edge and face sets stay `(cell, number)` field data and side regions are dropped, since mapping them to facets needs Volume A's numbering ([§2.2.11](#awaiting-a-licensed-run)); the Herrmann, bubble and generalized-plane-strain types come back as the default type of their shape, and a deck holds no data arrays.
  - **`szplt`** does not write polygon and polyhedral zones: TecIO 2018.3, the newest source available, refuses them in an SZL file, so the reader refuses them by name too and the face-based zones stay with the `.plt` and ASCII readers. Partitioned output is for tetrahedron and brick zones of a static file only (TecIO's own limit), and a series is written whole.
  - **`vtx`**: DOLFINx and ParaView read one cell type per file, so a file of several cell types or parts is meshio++'s own extension of the layout.
- **Generators the primitives leave out** — recorded when roadmap §3.1 closed (October 2026), with the [parametric primitives](./primitives.md#not-generated): generators driven by a user callback (no callback crosses the flat ABI; numpy users build `Mesh(points, cells)` directly, and [`isosurface`](./isosurface.md) on a `grid` covers implicit shapes); scanned or modelled assets (the bunny, the teapot, the bracket fixture — data rather than parameters, each under its own licence); and CSG combinations, under the volume-meshing non-goal below (surface booleans were reopened as a spike and kept out on its measurements, [findings](./spikes.md#surface-booleans-formerly-roadmap-7-8)) — two-body interface and contact fixtures are primitives placed with `transform` and joined with `merge`.
- **MPI in the library** — none planned ([C++ API](./cpp_api.md)); `partition`'s ghost layers produce the halo an MPI assembly in the owning application needs. ParMmg, MMG's MPI layer, is excluded with it.
- **Solver-coupled physics-ML** — assembled solver residuals, adjoints and Sobolev training, co-simulation, active-learning *labeling*, adaptive remeshing driven by a surrogate, and MPI model-part gathering. Every one needs a live solver (an assembly routine, its tangent, its communicator) and meshio++ has no notion of a discrete system; they belong in the application that owns the solver, the division [Symbolic and physics](physicsnemo/symbolic_and_physics.md) describes.
- **The Python-only layers stay Python** — `pmsh`, `zarr`, `cae` and `usd`, and the physics-ML surface (grids, point budgets, proximity graphs, datasets, training) are export targets and tooling for a training pipeline, registered in Python rather than the shared C++ registry. A core kernel they call (the neighbour search) does not change that; `tessellate` moved to the core ([tessellation](./tessellation.md)) because the native CLI and the flat bindings needed it, and its `Tessellation` helpers stay Python.
- **Polyscope in the release CLI executables** — excluded deliberately, so `view`/`screenshot` there report the build flag rather than opening a window ([viewer](./viewer.md)): the executable stays free of OpenGL, GLFW and X11. Narrowed in November 2026, once v18.0.0's plugins made it possible: a Polyscope plugin loaded beside the executable is queued as 4.8.2.
- **General volume meshing algorithms as native operations** — constrained tetrahedralization of a domain, hex-dominant meshing and general hex meshing from a CAD-like boundary, boolean/CSG, boundary-layer inflation and quadrangulation are each a library in their own right. (Narrowed in November 2026: geodesic distance, once listed here, is not one, since the heat method needs only the P1 operators and the sparse solver the core has, and shipped as [`geodesic_distance`](./geodesic.md); surface booleans were measured by a spike in November 2026 (exact in topology, but rounding its output to doubles left most curved results self-intersecting and coplanar contact unhandled, so no public operation is queued: [findings](./spikes.md#surface-booleans-formerly-roadmap-7-8)); and v18.0.0's provider registry is the optional-backend route below, which external meshers and field-aligned quads now take as installed programs: [`tetrahedralize`](./tetrahedralize.md#external-meshers-tetgen-and-gmsh)'s TetGen and Gmsh and [`remesh_quads`](./remesh_quads.md)'s QuadriFlow and Instant Meshes.) The answer is an optional backend (the KaHIP pattern), not a native implementation: volume domain meshing reaches meshio++ through [`tetrahedralize`](./tetrahedralize.md) (v17.23.0: natively by its octree backend, the skin fitted by isosurface stuffing, and exactly by Netgen, the skin kept) and adaptation through MMG ([MMG backend](./mmg.md)); boundary-layer inflation reopened through a design pass for layers grown from a surface ([`boundary_layer`](./boundary_layer.md), v17.23.0), the volume behind them filled by `tetrahedralize`; and quadrangulation reopened for CAD surfaces only (recombination of `mesh_cad_surface`'s own triangles by a matching, v17.23.0), field-aligned remeshing through installed programs ([`remesh_quads`](./remesh_quads.md)). A native constrained tetrahedralisation stays out: `orient3d` and `insphere` exist and the Delaunay kernel is short (Bowyer 1981, Watson 1981), but boundary recovery with Steiner points is where TetGen and the exact constructions spend their complexity (George, Hecht and Saltel 1991; Si and Gärtner 2005; Shewchuk 2008; Si 2015; Diazzi et al. 2023). Reopen it when a design pass shows a native recovery on the core's predicates within a stated size, measured against `tetrahedralize`'s probes and its Thingi10K sample. Two exceptions are in scope. Surface triangulation of a CAD model is native since v17.13.0 ([`mesh_cad_surface`](./mesh_cad.md)): the robust predicates it needs are Shewchuk's, public domain and a few hundred lines, and a planar constrained Delaunay triangulation per face is small enough to own; this reverses the earlier decision that dropped the queued Delaunay item, for surfaces and the plane only. Lattice generation is the other: [`voxelize`](./voxelize.md), [`octree`](./octree.md), [`remesh_volume`](./remesh_volume.md)'s isosurface stuffing and the octree volume mesher [`octree_mesh`](./octree_mesh.md) (stuffed tetrahedra and the all-hexahedral octree dual, uniform or graded, [plan](./octree_mesh_3d_plan.md)) are index arithmetic over a signed distance on a lattice, followed by projection and untangling, with no predicate to make robust.
- **A declared tolerance deciding degeneracy in `mesh_cad_surface`** — `model_tolerances` widens where uses start, how far corners and uses may lie apart and which trims pair, but whether an edge is degenerate (a pole) stays on the global tolerance: an edge shorter than a generous declared tolerance is real geometry, and collapsing it would change the model rather than accept its gaps ([item 21](./mesh_cad.md#how-it-works)).
- **What mesh generation leaves, by decision** — recorded when roadmap §6 closed (October 2026); [mesh generation](./mesh_cad.md) and its [measured-and-parked notes](./mesh_cad.md#measured-and-parked) hold the numbers:
  - **No small-angle bound stated on a curved face.** Corner lopping's bound (Pav and Walkington 2005) is the plane's; on a curved face `lop_corners` reports the angles it reaches and termination stays `max_points` and the insertion-radius declines. Stating one would need the paper's argument redone in the surface's metric.
  - **A pinched boundary stays declined** by the flattening of 6.4.4: splitting the pinch into one vertex per wedge makes the coarse mesh manifold, but the face's loops pair the pinch's edges otherwise than the wedges do, so each loop would have to be re-stitched through both copies.
  - **Field-aligned quads stay with backends.** Recombination of the mesher's own triangles leaves its worst quads at poles (0.12 on the sphere at `h = 0.1`); field-aligned remeshing is Instant Meshes (BSD-3), QuadriFlow (MIT) or QuadWild (GPL, excluded), each a library of its own. Since v18.0.0 that pattern is the provider registry, and [`remesh_quads`](./remesh_quads.md) runs an installed QuadriFlow or Instant Meshes as a separate process (0.25 to 0.58 on the same sphere, by seed); a linked add-on wheel extracting their solvers stays unqueued until a user needs them in the wheels or the browser. The core's cross field and quad layout (`detail/cross_field`, `detail/quad_layout`) stay `fit_cad`'s.
  - **Curved boundary layers: linearize first.** `boundary_layer` takes linear triangles and quads; a `triangle6` or `quad9` surface is linearized first. Growing curved layers needs a Bernstein bound on a prism's and a hexahedron's Jacobian (the tensor bound `detail/curved_quad` has for quads, raised a dimension) to keep "no cell is written folded" true, and its mid-edge nodes' directions; no model has asked.
  - **Mesh-generation corpora with non-commercial terms stay out.** The Fusion 360 Gallery (Willis et al. 2021) permits use for non-commercial research only and asks its users to indemnify Autodesk, which an MIT project's published measurements cannot accept.
  - **No numpy twin** of `mesh_cad_surface`, `triangulate_polygon_2d` or `tetrahedralize`: a second implementation of the predicates and the triangulation could not stay byte-identical, the precedent of [`remesh_volume`](./remesh_volume.md).
  - **Healing the model.** Sewing gaps, removing sliver faces or rebuilding pcurves changes the CAD model, which is the CAD kernel's work; `mesh_cad_surface` meshes around defects without writing them back. Narrowed in November 2026: the CAD kernel is reachable through the provider registry, so healing at read time, before the B-rep reaches the mesher, is the STEP and IGES readers' `heal` option ([STEP reading](./formats/step.md#reading)); `mesh_cad_surface` itself still never heals.
  - **Code from Triangle, TetGen, Gmsh, CGAL's GPL packages or Blossom V**, under any route other than a separate process: their licences ([THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES](https://github.com/loumalouomega/meshioplusplus/blob/main/THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md)). Geogram's constrained tetrahedralisation is in that list too: it creates TetGen's AGPL Delaunay kernel. The separate-process route is `tetrahedralize(backend="tetgen")` and `backend="gmsh"` ([external meshers](./tetrahedralize.md#external-meshers-tetgen-and-gmsh): installed executables behind named tet-mesher providers, never linked), and the rule keeps the gmsh Python API, which runs in-process, out of the interop bridges (4.10).
  - **A native parallel Delaunay kernel** in the style of HXT (Marot, Pellerin and Remacle 2019): it is the fast path of a 3-D mesher the core does not have, and the 2-D triangulation of one face is small enough that faces in parallel already use every thread on a real model.
  - **Advancing-front surface meshing as a second native mesher** (Lo 1985, Peraire et al. 1987, Löhner and Parikh 1988, Schöberl 1997): smoother gradation, but its robustness rests on the front's intersection tests that the Delaunay route avoids, and two native meshers would double what must stay byte-identical. Frontal point placement inside the Delaunay mesher (Rebay 1993, Tristano, Owen and Canann 1998) is the alternative if the varying metric's circumcentres leave triangles poorly aligned on strongly stretched faces.
- **Contact mechanics itself** — penalty or mortar assembly, friction and contact search in time need a live solver. meshio++ detects and exports the interface of one snapshot ([interfaces and contact](./region_adjacency.md)); the contact algorithm belongs in the application that owns the solver.
- **MMG in the base wheel and in the native CLI executables** — it is LGPL-3.0 (GPL-3.0 with the ELAS that `move` needs) and an external install, so the native backend is linked dynamically into builds that opt in, and its operations throw by name where it is absent ([MMG backend](./mmg.md#licence)). v18.0.0 narrowed this for Python: the `meshioplusplus-mmg` add-on wheel ships it, built without ELAS, beside the MIT base wheel ([installation](./installation.md#what-the-pypi-wheels-contain)); the same split for the native CLI, a plugin beside the executable, is queued as 4.8.1. Its WebAssembly half was lifted for the full artifact when roadmap 3.1.8 closed (October 2026): MMG is linked there statically, without ELAS, under the LGPL-3.0 relinking terms the artifact's notices state ([WebAssembly](./wasm.md#mmg-in-the-full-artifact)); the lean artifact keeps none.
- **What the full WebAssembly build leaves, by decision** — recorded when roadmap §5 closed (October 2026); [fit CAD's measured and parked notes](./fit_cad.md#measured-and-parked) hold the numbers and the reopening conditions:
  - **Constraints beyond the coaxial, right-angle and equal-radius ones.** Parallel but offset axes are different surfaces, and a bolt circle or a plane at another angle needs the pattern known to the pass, so `constrain_primitives` stays exact only where it is exact.
  - **G1 at valence 5 and 6 beyond the sweeps' reach.** The refine-before-discard step of the patch network brings the largest G1 angle down on the Catmull-Clark fixtures ([limitations](./fit_cad.md#limitations)); a construction that holds G1 exactly there (Loop and Schaefer's full scheme, or G-splines) is not implemented.
  - **Genus-one-and-more homology cuts.** The layout's pieces are triangle groups, so a cut along both sides of a loop has no representation; that is a change to the layout's partition, not a bounded experiment.
  - **THB stays experimental**, behind its knot-line fallback: a rung whose hierarchical fit misses its target falls back to knot lines, so `"thb"` never leaves as facets a patch `"knots"` fits.
- **KaHIP in the lean WASM build, ADIOS2 and TecIO in either WASM build** — KaHIP ships only in the full artifact (serial kaffpa, built by `build-wasm-deps.sh --full`; a graph partitioner would bloat every consumer's bundle, so the lean pair keeps the SFC method with `"kahip"` throwing by name as the contract); no one has made a wasm build of ADIOS2 (`vtx`); TecIO's redistribution terms keep `szplt` out. Each reports by name in the lean and the full artifact alike ([what stays out](./wasm.md#what-stays-out)).
- **zstd/lz4 codecs in the lean WASM build** — the lean artifact keeps zlib as its only block codec; the full one (v17.2.0) links zstd, lz4 and bzip2. A zstd-compressed VTK XML file is a meshio++ extension VTK itself has no compressor for, so vtk.js does not read it; the second reader of a zstd file is meshio++'s native and Python ones. **Memory mapping and the Kokkos backend under Emscripten** — CMake refuses the Kokkos backend under Emscripten, and there is nothing to map inside MEMFS.
- **Single-file output for DOLFIN, TetGen and EnSight from WASM**, or lifting DOLFIN's simplicial restriction — both are facts of the formats; `writeMesh`'s written-paths return value (v11.2.0) is the fix for the bookkeeping they cause.
- **NURBS patches and trims as a second entity kind inside `Mesh`** — decided with the [isogeometric layer](./iga.md) (v16.43.0, [findings](./spikes.md#the-isogeometric-layer)): a model (`NurbsInfo`, `BrepInfo`) stays a side channel beside the mesh, with its own evaluate, refine and sample operations, because a first-class entity kind needs patches, control nets, weights, knots and trimming curves in `Mesh`, every mesh backend (MESHIO, NATIVE, KRATOS), every binding and the C++ ABI: a major version for a gain the three operations already give. Reopen it when an operation must act on patches and trims as mesh entities (mixed IGA and FEM assembly in one model, for instance, or a patch-aware `partition`), and start from the spike's list of what the side channel cannot do: no `Mesh` operation sees a patch, the model does not travel through the pipeline, and an MFEM model refines every direction alike.
- **Polyhedron blocks as a WASM gap** — `vtu`, `ensight`, `cgns`, `med` and `openfoam` all write them from WASM; a consumer that cannot is constrained by its own data model.
- **Performance work measured or decided against** — recorded when the roadmap's performance section closed (October 2026), and not re-proposed when it reopened as [§5](#performance) in November 2026; a new performance change passes the gates of [landing a performance change](./benchmarks.md#landing-a-performance-change):
  - **The serial phases kept for determinism**: the keep-first weld loop (chains make it order-dependent), the union sweeps of `split` and `compute_normals` (cheap, and their partitions are order-independent anyway), and `remesh`'s energy sweep (every accepted move changes what the next test reads; it has no numpy twin because a near-tie decided differently yields a different clustering).
  - **A facet table cached across pipeline steps** — measured ([benchmarks](./benchmarks.md#pipeline-row-what-a-shared-facet-table-could-save)): in a `Smooth` then `ExtractSurface` chain a cache saves at most one table build, about 3.6% of the chain on SEQ at tier L with the default ten iterations, at most 10% on four OpenMP threads and about 20% with one iteration. `smooth` and `extract_surface` do not share a record structure, so it would also need a refactor, and a cache on the `Mesh` is a Tier A layout change. Reopen it for a workload that chains several facet readers after cheap topology-preserving steps; the `pipeline_smooth_surface` row measures it.
  - **Explicit SIMD intrinsics or `-march` flags** — portability across wheels, WASM and the release binaries is worth more than the scalar kernels cost; revisit only if the harness shows a kernel dominating.
  - **Kokkos device execution** — every `parallel_for` body captures host pointers (`parallel.hpp`); the GPU route is the DLPack/CuPy handoff ([GPU handoff](./gpu.md)).
  - **An occupancy pyramid over the distance query's bucket grid** (formerly roadmap 5.3.1) — measured in November 2026 and not taken: descending the grid's occupancy at coarser block sizes, best first with Euclidean bounds, was 2 to 3 times faster than the Chebyshev-shell search at tier L, but a bounding-volume hierarchy of the triangles (formerly 5.3.2, which had been decided against because its visit order was thought to make the equidistant-triangle tiebreak observable, a reason the total order on (distance², triangle id) answers) was a further 2 to 4 times faster with its build included, on uniform and graded targets alike, and replaced the grid in ABI 40 ([benchmarks](./benchmarks.md#a-bounding-volume-hierarchy-for-the-distance-query-and-a-grid-for-find_interface)). A cell's bound is the cell's, so a point far from the surface still opens every bucket of a patch around its nearest one; a box is as tight as its triangles.
  - **Tuning the pure-Python fallback readers** — the fix is making the C++ path accept the file, not a faster fallback; `_decimate.py`'s heap-based twin is deleted once `decimate` accepts its inputs, not optimised.
  - **Dropping the copy in `transform`** — `transform` copies the connectivity and data it does not change (through `transform_owned_copy`, as 17 operation files copy through `clone_mesh`, `clone_geometry` or `quality_clone_mesh`), and the Python twin copies too; that copy is what makes the result a new mesh that aliases nothing, so removing it is an API change, not an optimisation.
  - **Caching between the browser viewer's applies** — every apply re-reads the staged file and replays the whole pipeline, which is what makes undo exact. Measured with the typed-array channel ([benchmarks](./benchmarks.md#a-typed-array-channel-for-the-browser-viewer)), the read is at most 2 to 25% of an apply that smooths and up to 69% of a quality-only apply on a large surface; a cached mesh or pipeline prefix would hold a second copy of the mesh in the 32-bit heap MEMFS already shares, and make undo depend on invalidation.

- **Code from AGPL-3.0 scan tools** — [ScanRuler](https://github.com/CNCKitchen/ScanRuler) and its STEP importer meshStep are AGPL-3.0, which this MIT library cannot take in any form: no copying, porting, translating or paraphrasing of their code, tests, shaders, guides or data. §7 was listed from their published feature descriptions only; an agent implementing an item works from the literature it cites and from meshio++'s own kernels, does not open their source, and does not use their output files as fixtures. The same rule as for Triangle, TetGen and Gmsh above, without the separate-process exception, since nothing in §7 needs to run them.
- **An interactive parametric CAD modeller** — a sketch constraint solver, a feature history with rebuilds and rollback, face fingerprints that survive upstream edits, thread and insert catalogues: a CAD application, not a mesh library. meshio++ fits and writes geometry ([`fit_cad`](./fit_cad.md), 7.6.5) and may build a solid from a scripted list of profile features through the CAD provider (7.7.4); editing that solid belongs to the CAD tool that opens the STEP file.
- **Terminal rendering stays software, dependency-free and core-only** — no GPU, no windowing stack, no new dependency and no audio library; it is a *preview*, not a replacement for Polyscope or vtk.js, and does not try to match their fidelity, lighting or picking. Python calls the core or raises by name (the [`remesh_volume`](./remesh_volume.md) precedent: a NumPy edge-function rasterizer would be slow and double the surface that has to stay byte-identical). Vector output is unchanged: SVG, TikZ and glTF keep the painter's algorithm and their byte-pinned twins, and their colormap tables are only ever extended. The interactive loop is not a library feature (raw mode, signals and the event loop live in the CLI layer; the library exposes frames and encoders, never a tty, and WASM and the C API carry frames and text only). `--follow` polls modification times and does not use inotify or kqueue. The soundtrack is generated, played by an external player and silent unless asked for, in CI and over SSH. See [terminal rendering](./tui.md#design-decisions).
