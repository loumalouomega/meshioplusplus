# Volume meshes on an octree — `octree_mesh`

`octree_mesh` meshes the inside of a skin on a balanced octree. The skin is any surface (`triangle`, `quad` and `polygon` blocks, read from STL, OBJ, PLY or any other format; the boundary of a volume mesh is taken), closed or slightly dirty: inside and outside come from the fast generalised winding number, so holes, self-intersections and non-manifold edges are tolerated. It is the volume-meshing sibling of [`octree`](octree.md), which builds the tree around a skin without meshing its inside; `octree` itself is unchanged.

```python
import meshioplusplus as mp

skin = mp.read("bracket.stl")
cells, report = mp.octree_mesh(skin, output="castellated", min_size=0.5, max_size=4.0, return_report=True)
mp.write("bracket_cells.vtu", cells)
```

There are three outputs ([plan](octree_mesh_3d_plan.md)):

| `output` | what it is |
|---|---|
| `"castellated"` | the leaves inside the skin as `hexahedron` cells, with surrogate-boundary tags, for embedded and Shifted Boundary Method solvers |
| `"tet"` (default) | conforming graded tetrahedra fitted to the skin, sharp features preserved |
| `"hex"` | all-hexahedra: a grid at the skin size, made manifold, pillowed, fitted to the skin and untangled |

## The size field

Leaves are no larger than the size field `h(x) = clamp(min(h_max, h_curv, h_feat, h_gap, h_user), min_size, max_size)`, evaluated on every skin triangle as the smallest size its vertices ask for:

| option | term |
|---|---|
| `max_size` | the size away from the skin (default: an eighth of the skin's bounding-box diagonal) |
| `curvature_angle` | `h_curv = 2 sin(θ/2) / κ_max`: a leaf edge subtends at most `θ` degrees on the skin's largest principal curvature (default 15; 0 disables) |
| `feature_angle`, `feature_size` | `h_feat` along edges sharper than `feature_angle` degrees (default 30) and non-manifold edges (default: `min_size`) |
| `gap_cells` | `h_gap = t / gap_cells`, `t` the distance an inward ray from a skin vertex travels to the far wall (default 2; 0 disables); `gap_outside` also resolves the gaps between separate parts |
| `size_array` | `h_user`, a `point_data` array of target sizes on the skin |
| `min_size` | the floor (default `max_size / 32`) |

`min_size` and `max_size` only clamp the field: a sphere meshed with `min_size=0.01` is still as coarse as its curvature asks for (about 0.26 at the default 15° on a unit sphere). To refine, tighten a criterion (`curvature_angle`, `feature_size`, `size_array`) or, under uniform grading, set `surface_size`.

The root lattice has `L = ceil(log2(max_size / min_size))` levels (at most 19; `max_level` caps it) with root cells of edge `min_size · 2^L`, centred on the padded box (`padding`, `padding_relative`, or `bounds`). A leaf the skin cuts (exact triangle–box overlap) is split until it is no larger than the size its triangles ask for, every other leaf until it is no larger than `max_size`; then `grading_layers` rings of leaves around every cut leaf are kept at its level, and the tree is 2:1-balanced across faces, edges and corners. `skin_grading="uniform"` puts every cut leaf at one level instead (`surface_size`, by default the smallest size the field asks for on the skin), with two rings around it and at least three skin sizes of padding.

## Inside and outside

A point is inside where its generalised winding number exceeds `winding_threshold` (0.5). The winding number is evaluated with the tree approximation of Barill et al. (2018): far triangles are replaced by their area-weighted dipoles and first and second moments when they are more than `winding_beta` (2) times their extent away, which gives about three correct digits; values within 0.1 of the threshold are evaluated again at a quarter of the error. `winding_beta=0` sums every triangle exactly. A skin wound inside out is detected by its negative volume, its winding numbers negated, and a warning reported.

**A closed skin is flooded rather than evaluated.** When every edge of the welded skin is walked as often one way as the other (a closed, consistently oriented skin, whose winding number is an integer everywhere off it), the leaves the skin does not reach are joined into regions through their faces (each leaf tested against every skin triangle with its box grown by a millionth of a finest cell, since a skin lying exactly on a leaf face, such as a cap on a lattice plane, can escape the cut flags' closed test and would let a region cross it), and each region takes one winding number, evaluated at its three lowest *deep* leaves — leaves that neither they nor any of their 26 neighbours is cut — which must round to the same integer. A point in a deep leaf takes that integer, and only the points beside the skin are evaluated. The margin of a leaf keeps a cut flag rounded the wrong way at a shared face away from it, and every decision that reads a winding number's value (the near-threshold rechecks, the stuffing's cuts, the hexahedral repair's scores) still reads an evaluated one, so the output is byte-identical to evaluating every point: a uniformly fine castellated mesh of a sphere at `h = 0.025` (283 409 points) takes 18.1 s instead of 30.3, the bunny at `h = 1.5` 10.2 s instead of 22.4, and the stuffing of a uniform tetrahedral sphere 4.7 s instead of 9.1 (four threads; [benchmarks](benchmarks.md#flooded-inside-test-in-octree_mesh)). A skin that is not closed, or a `winding_threshold` within 0.1 of an integer, evaluates every point as before. `num_winding_skipped` counts the points that were not evaluated.

## The castellated output

The kept leaves (`fill`: `"inside"`, the default, those whose centre is inside; `"cover"`, those and every leaf the skin cuts; `"all"`) are one `hexahedron` block in Morton order, with:

| name | kind | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `inside` | cell region | the cells whose centre is inside |
| `boundary_cells` | cell region | the cells the skin passes through |
| `surrogate_boundary` | side region | the faces of inside cells with no inside cell across them: the surrogate boundary of the Shifted Boundary Method |
| `sdf:distance` | point data | the signed distance to the skin (negative inside, 0 on it) |
| `sdf:closest_point` | point data, 3 components | the nearest point on the skin |
| `octree:hanging` | point data | 1 for a node in the interior of a face or edge of a cell |
| `octree:level`, `octree:cut` | cell data | as for [`octree`](octree.md) |

![Castellated cells of a sphere, cut open and coloured by octree level](/images/octree_mesh_castellated.png)

An inside leaf beside a finer leaf that is not inside is split until every face of the surrogate boundary is a whole face of an inside cell, so the side region describes it exactly; the surrogate faces enclose the inside cells' volume (the tests check the divergence theorem). `attach_distance=False` skips the two `sdf:*` arrays.

## The tetrahedral output

The default output is a conforming, graded tetrahedral mesh of the skin's inside, in three stages.

**The lattice.** The balanced octree is tetrahedralised as a graded body-centred cubic (BCC) lattice on the doubled integer lattice, so every vertex is exact: each leaf is a cone from its centre over fans of its face portions (a face shared with four finer leaves is four portions), and across a face between two leaves of the same size the two cones become the BCC tetrahedra around the segment between the centres. Six shapes occur, with dihedral angles between 35.26° and 125.26°; a region of equal leaves is all BCC tetrahedra (60°–90°). This is our own construction after Labelle and Shewchuk's lattice, and the tests pin its angle table.

**Stuffing.** Isosurface stuffing (Labelle and Shewchuk, SIGGRAPH 2007) cuts the lattice to the skin: a lattice edge whose ends lie on either side gets a cut point where it crosses the skin (exactly, through the winding-number tree, or by bisection where it passes through a hole); a lattice vertex closer to a cut point than `alpha_short` (edges between a leaf centre and another vertex) or `alpha_long` (the others) times the edge is *warped* onto it; then every tetrahedron the skin crosses is cut by a stencil. Quadrilaterals take the diagonal through their lower-numbered lattice vertex on lattice faces and their shorter diagonal on the skin, so both sides of every face agree. With the paper's `alpha_long = 0.24799` and `alpha_short = 0.40173`, every tetrahedron of a uniform lattice has dihedral angles between 10.7° and 164.8°; the tests check this on spheres, tori and random ellipsoids. The lattice's own boundary is never warped, so `keep_outside=True`, which also keeps the tetrahedra outside the skin as the `outside` region, fills the root lattice exactly.

![Graded tetrahedra of a sphere, cut open and coloured by the level of their leaf](/images/octree_mesh_tets.png)

**Features.** With `preserve_features` (the default), the skin's edges sharper than `feature_angle` form a graph of corners (where it branches, ends, or turns by more than `corner_angle`) and chains between them. Each corner moves the nearest boundary node within reach onto itself, exactly; each chain is traced on the boundary by a shortest path in a tube around it, whose nodes move onto the chain. Every move is refused if it would invert a tetrahedron the output keeps or create a dihedral angle below 2° there that was not already there; the slivers outside the skin, which the output drops (unless `keep_outside`), do not count, since on fine graded skins they used to hold a rim's nodes off its chain (a capped cylinder of radius 0.5 missed all four rim chains at `min_size = 0.1` and recovers all of them now, at 0.2, 0.1, 0.05 and 0.025). A move goes in four quarter steps and keeps the steps that succeeded. A corner or chain that cannot be recovered is counted (`num_corners_snapped`, `num_feature_chains_missed`) and reported in `warnings`. The output carries the `feature_corners` and `feature_edges` point regions.

![A cube meshed with its corners and edges kept, coloured by each tetrahedron's smallest dihedral angle](/images/octree_mesh_cube.png)

`conformity="cleaving"` meshes nested shells (a skin whose winding number reaches 2 or more, such as a sphere inside a sphere; the hexahedral output does too, [below](#the-hexahedral-output)): the interface at each winding level `winding_threshold + k` is stuffed in turn through the tetrahedra between the previous interfaces, with the earlier interfaces held fixed, so the materials conform. It follows the idea of lattice cleaving (Bronson, Levine and Whitaker, IEEE TVCG 2014), one interface at a time rather than with its multi-material stencils. The materials are the `octree_mesh:material` cell data and the `material_<k>` cell regions.

| name | kind | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `inside` | cell region | the tetrahedra inside the skin |
| `outside` | cell region | `keep_outside`: the tetrahedra outside it |
| `boundary` | side region | the boundary faces |
| `feature_corners`, `feature_edges` | point regions | the nodes on the skin's corners and feature chains |
| `material_<k>` | cell regions | cleaving: each material |
| `octree:level` | cell data | the level of the leaf a tetrahedron came from |
| `octree_mesh:shape` | cell data | the lattice shape (1–6), 0 for a tetrahedron stuffing cut |
| `octree_mesh:material` | cell data | the material, when there is more than one |
| `sdf:distance` | point data | the signed distance to the skin |

**Optimisation.** `fit_iterations` rounds (default 5) then improve the mesh: an optimal-Delaunay (ODT) relocation of the interior nodes (the pass of [`smooth`](smooth.md)), a slide of every skin node towards the mean of its skin neighbours projected back onto the skin, and the 2-3 and 3-2 flips of [`optimize_volume`](optimize_volume.md). With `dihedral_floor_guard` (the default) a node is put back when it would lower the mesh's smallest dihedral angle or raise its largest, and a round of flips is kept only when it does neither, so optimisation never makes the worst angles worse; feature nodes, the box and interfaces between materials never move, and flips are made only for one material. `num_optimized_moves` and `num_flips` count what changed.

![Histograms of the dihedral angles of a sphere's tetrahedra before and after optimisation, graded and uniform](/images/octree_mesh_dihedrals.png)

`guarantee_applies` in the report is true when the 10.7°–164.8° bound covers the whole mesh: uniform skin grading, no warp refused, and no node moved onto a feature. What the stuffing trades for the bound is the boundary, which is the skin's within `hausdorff_to_skin` rather than its triangles: on the unit sphere's skin from [`mesh_cad_surface`](./mesh_cad.md#volume-meshes) under uniform grading at `surface_size = h`, it is within 0.036 `h`, 0.028 `h` and 0.03 `h` at `h` = 0.2, 0.1 and 0.05, and the enclosed volume the skin's within 0.30%, 0.075% and 0.019%. Otherwise the actual angles are in `min_dihedral` and `max_dihedral`; snapping to a sharp corner can lower them locally, and graded skins cut transition shapes.

## The hexahedral output

`output="hex"` gives a mesh of hexahedra only, in four stages.

**The grid.** With `hex_grading="uniform"` (the default), the cells of edge `h` (the skin size: `surface_size`, or the smallest size the field asks for on the skin, rounded down to a level of the frame) whose centre is inside are kept, one grid everywhere. `max_cells` caps its cell count. With `hex_grading="graded"` the hexahedra follow the octree's grading instead (below).

**Manifold repair.** Kept cells may touch along an edge or at a corner only, which no hexahedral mesh with a manifold boundary can have. Around every grid vertex the kept and the empty cells of its 2×2×2 block must each be face-connected; where they are not, the empty cell with the largest winding number is added, until every block passes. The boundary is then a closed 2-manifold of quadrilaterals (the tests check every block by brute force and the boundary's volume by the divergence theorem).

**Pillow and fit.** With `pillow` (the default), a layer of hexahedra goes along the boundary (Mitchell and Tautges 1995), so no hexahedron has two faces on the skin: each boundary node gets a copy on the skin, and the node itself moves half a cell inside. The copy goes where the line through the node along the boundary's own normal (the sum of the area vectors of its quadrilaterals, smoothed once over its neighbours) first meets a part of the skin facing the same way, within half a cell of the closest point's distance; where that line misses, it takes the closest point. The closest point alone ignores which way the boundary faces: a node by an edge or a corner of the skin is about as near two or three of its faces, and a face's copies went to the side face and flattened the pillow cells (a unit box at `min_size = 0.05`, graded, with features off had 383 corners of zero scaled Jacobian). The node moves inside along the same direction, but no more than 0.45 of the way to the far wall of the skin, so a part thinner than a cell does not fold. Without the pillow, the boundary nodes move onto the skin themselves, and cells with several faces on the skin can stay poor. With `preserve_features`, each feature corner takes the nearest boundary node within 1.5 cells exactly, and each chain the shortest path between its corners' nodes on the boundary's quadrilateral edges, in a tube of 1.25 cells around it; the path's nodes go to the chain points at the same fraction of the chain's length as they are of the path's, so they stay in order and apart, and the pillow nodes of snapped copies follow them. A path on a staircase turns inside a quadrilateral (two of its edges at one corner): snapping those three nodes onto one chain segment would put them on a line and flatten it, so the middle node is released from the chain and slides along the skin within 0.08 cells of it. A chain shorter than the cell there is not snapped, nor a corner that only such chains end at: on a scan most "creases" are noise a cell cannot follow (24 of the bunny's 28 chains at `feature_angle=60` and `min_size=1.5`), and pinning them held the cells around them flat or inverted. The warnings count them, `num_feature_chains_missed` includes them, and the size field and the tetrahedral output still see every feature.

**Untangling.** `untangle_iterations` sweeps (default 50) raise each node's worst corner scaled Jacobian: every node below 0.6 tries the mean of its edge neighbours, the mean of its trilinear reflections (the position that cancels the twist of each hexahedron around it), and steps of a fifth of its mean edge, halving every ten sweeps: along the six axes, or, below 0.2, along the 26 directions of a cube with a doubling or halving line search. It takes the best candidate that raises its worst corner, or that keeps it and raises the sum of the corners around it, capped at 0.6 (a hexahedron with poor corners at two nodes keeps its minimum when either moves alone). A sweep that moves nothing while a corner is below 0.2 starts the steps again, once. The skin nodes slide along the skin, projected along their boundary normal as they were placed, and the feature nodes are held. If a corner is still below 0.05, the feature nodes of every hexahedron below it are released, each within 0.08 cells of its chain or corner, and a fifth of the sweeps runs again; the warnings count them. The sweeps run in ascending node order, so the result does not depend on the thread count.

**The body-fitted fit.** When the fit above leaves a corner below 0.2, the fit of version 18.0.0 (closest points, the six-direction search, no repair) is run as well and the one with the larger smallest corner is kept, with a warning when it is the old one: a skin the fit handles worse than its predecessor still gets the predecessor's mesh.

**Materials.** With `conformity="cleaving"`, nested shells (a skin whose winding number reaches 2 or more, such as a sphere inside a sphere) become materials, as in the tetrahedral output: a hexahedron's material is `floor(w - winding_threshold) + 1` for its winding number `w` (at most 16). Each skin triangle belongs to the shell of the material just inside it. For each level `L` from 2, the hexahedra of material `L` or more keep off the nodes of everything outside material `L - 1` and of the boundary, so each interface lies inside, a layer from the next, and are made manifold around every node by adding hexahedra of material `L - 1` (the largest winding number first), else by giving up their own (the smallest first). Each interface is then pillowed on both sides: every interface node gets a node on the shell and a copy inside, the inner hexahedra move onto the copies, and every interface face gets one hexahedron of each material, so no hexahedron has two faces on the interface. The fit places the shell nodes on their own shell (projected along the interface's normal and onto that shell's triangles only) and the two sides a pillow's depth away, no more than half way to their nearest neighbour on that side (a graded interface lies half a cell inside its shell), snaps each shell's own features, and untangles with every shell node sliding on its shell. The outer boundary projects onto the outer shell only. The body-fitted fit has no interfaces, so cleaving keeps the fit above. A skin with one shell gives the same bytes as without cleaving. `hausdorff_to_skin` then measures the boundary and the interfaces against the skin.

| name | kind | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `inside` | cell region | every hexahedron |
| `boundary` | side region | the boundary faces (with `pillow`, the outer face, facet 5, of each pillow cell) |
| `feature_corners`, `feature_edges` | point regions | the nodes on the skin's corners and feature chains |
| `octree:level` | cell data | the grid's level (graded: the finest level around the hexahedron's centre) |
| `octree_mesh:pillow` | cell data | 1 for a pillow cell (an interface's layers too) |
| `material_<k>` | cell regions | cleaving: each material |
| `octree_mesh:material` | cell data | cleaving: the material, when there is more than one |
| `sdf:distance` | point data | the signed distance to the skin |

**Graded hexahedra.** `hex_grading="graded"` carries the octree's grading into the hexahedra by the dual method of Livesu, Pitzalis and Cherchi ("Optimal dual schemes for adaptive grid based hexmeshing", ACM TOG 41(2), 2022), with the scheme detection, marking and orientation ported from [CinoLib](https://github.com/mlivesu/cinolib) (MIT) and the pairing of Pitzalis, Livesu, Cherchi, Gobbetti and Scateni ("Generalized adaptive refinement for grid-based hexahedral meshing", ACM TOG 40(6), 2021). The octree is paired (the roots split once, and every leaf with a split sibling split, until the eight children of every parent are all leaves or all split) and stays balanced across faces, edges and corners. A transition vertex is the centre of a face of a parent whose children are leaves, when the leaves across that face are one level finer; every coarse leaf around the transitions is replaced by one of the eight schemes for strongly balanced grids (flat, flat beside a convex turn, one to three convex turns, the concave edge, and the centre and side of a concave corner), placed by the transition vertices it holds. Coordinates are integers on a lattice of a twentieth of the finest cell, so the schemes weld by exact keys and every rotation is a signed permutation. The result is a conforming polyhedral mesh whose every interior vertex has eight cells meeting as a cube's corners, so its dual is all hexahedra: one per interior vertex, with a corner at the centroid of each cell around it. Both properties are checked, and a configuration the port does not cover is refused by name (`Unsupported`), never meshed wrong; the five schemes for weakly balanced grids are not ported. A dual hexahedron is kept when the vertex it surrounds is more than a tenth of its cell inside the boundary: a vertex on a skin that lies on a lattice plane, such as a cylinder's cap, would otherwise be kept by a hair and pressed flat by the pillow. A vertex that close to the skin is dropped only when the skin there is the boundary: some point a tenth of its cell away along an axis, or just past its nearest skin point, is outside (for a vertex on the skin itself, along the cube's diagonals, off the lattice's planes). A nested shell inside is not the boundary: dropping the vertices beside it opened a cavity there, pillowed and folded, with or without cleaving (two nested spheres, graded at `min_size = 0.1` without cleaving, ended at a smallest corner scaled Jacobian of −0.99 with the fit of 18.0.0 and −0.02 with one inverted hexahedron with the fit above, and reach 0.47 now). On the skin itself a winding number reads half-way between its sides only up to the sign of a zero, and is taken as the middle of the diagonal probes' range where it disagrees by more than a quarter. The manifold repair works on the vertex stars of the unstructured mesh (the empty hexahedron with the largest winding number first), and the pillow, the feature snap and the untangler use each node's own size, its mean edge along the boundary. The raw dual's transition hexahedra have corners down to a scaled Jacobian of 0.28. `octree:level` is the finest level of the leaves around each hexahedron's centre.

| skin | uniform: hexahedra, min. scaled Jacobian, Hausdorff | graded: hexahedra, min. scaled Jacobian, Hausdorff |
|---|---|---|
| sphere, `curvature_angle=5`, `max_size=0.5` | 22 128, 0.53, 0.0015 | 18 281, 0.28, 0.0014 |
| capped cylinder (radius 0.5, height 2), `min_size=0.05` | 16 472, 0.53, 0.0030 | 7 461, 0.28, 0.0051 |

No hexahedron of the first three has a non-positive volume. The savings grow with the room the tree has to coarsen: 55% on the cylinder, whose skin grades from its rims to its caps, 17% on the finely curved sphere and 9% on the thin torus, whose tube leaves little inside to coarsen. Pairing and the balance across corners refine more than the castellated output's tree, and the boundary layer is as fine as the uniform grid's. The graded smallest corner, 0.28, is the raw dual's own transition hexahedra. On the scanned bunny 24 of the 28 chains are shorter than a cell and not snapped; the graded output keeps every corner at 0.28 or more and misses one more chain, which the uniform grid recovers, hence its larger Hausdorff distance. The uniform output keeps two pillow cells around one inner node with a corner of zero (five cells down to −0.006 before short creases were left out, 41 down to −0.16 in 18.0.0), which the warnings report: the skin there is more than two cells thick, and no single move of that node raises them. A coarser uniform bunny (`surface_size=3`) keeps four, one of them with a non-positive volume (five in 18.0.0). Harder scans show the same limit at scale: the decimated Happy Buddha and XYZ RGB dragon of [common-3d-test-models](https://github.com/alecjacobson/common-3d-test-models), at `min_size` 0.6% and `max_size` 3.2% of the diagonal with `feature_angle=60`, keep 283 and 254 cells with a non-positive corner under the uniform grid (2 063 and 4 819 in 18.0.0) and 102 and 66 graded (1 659 and 4 385), of which 63, 48, 17 and 9 have a non-positive volume (192, 416, 143 and 339). Their scans' creases are almost all shorter than a cell (962 of the Buddha's 1 117 chains, 1 954 of the dragon's 2 080), so the boundary no longer holds them, and the dragon's Hausdorff distance grows from 2.05 and 2.00 to 3.19 and 3.70 (the Buddha's stays within 0.0026). About a third of what remains lies where the skin is thinner than two cells, which the warnings say, naming the size to lower; the rest is where it creases or curves more tightly than a cell, which no fit within `min_size` follows ([measured and parked](#measured-and-parked)).

With features off (`feature_angle=0`, or `preserve_features=False` without `feature_size`), nothing holds the boundary to the skin's sharp edges: every corner stays positive, but the untangler rounds the edges (a unit box at `min_size = 0.05` is within 0.014 of its skin uniform and 0.037 graded, against 0.009 and 0.069 in 18.0.0).

The report's `min_scaled_jacobian` is the smallest over the cells, `surface_level` is the grid's level (graded: the finest level present), and `num_optimized_moves` counts the untangler's moves; a smallest corner scaled Jacobian below 0.2, or a corner left inverted, is reported in `warnings`. On a sphere, a torus and a cube the tests find it above 0.4 with the pillow (0.5 on the cube), every corner and edge kept, the boundary closed and within 0.05 of the skin; on the capped cylinder and on a box with features on or off, at or above 0.2 with no flat corner; and on two and three nested spheres under cleaving, in both gradings, at or above 0.2, conforming, with each material within 3% of its shell's volume. A box inside a box with both on the lattice's planes, graded, is the one nested skin the tests know to keep a few corners below zero (four inverted of 8 893 hexahedra, where the interface meets the dual's transitions at one corner); the uniform grid meshes it at 0.55.

### Measured and parked

Each was implemented or tried against the cells the hexahedral output keeps with a non-positive corner on scans (formerly roadmap 7.9.2, November 2026), measured, and left out. Those cells are a limit of the output, reported by the warnings above, which name how many lie where the skin is thinner than two cells and the size to lower. Worth measuring again with a change to the grid's connectivity, which none of these makes.

- **A skin smoothed before the fit.** Fitting the hexahedral boundary to a copy of the skin smoothed by Taubin's filter, so that a crease or a curve tighter than a cell is not followed, does not remove them. The decimated Happy Buddha at `min_size` 0.6% of its diagonal, uniform, with features off, keeps 168; smoothed by 3, 10, 30 and 100 iterations (`smooth(method="taubin", preserve_features=False, guard_inversion=False)`) it keeps 132, 101, 149 and 114, never steadily fewer. The Stanford bunny at `min_size=1.5` and `feature_angle=60` goes from two cells to 30 and 61 after 2 and 5 iterations.
- **The boundary let off the skin.** [`untangle`](./untangle.md) on the Buddha's output removes 14 of the 168 with the boundary nodes fixed and 137 with them free, at a smallest corner near −0.96 and with the boundary off the skin. A relief stage after the repair, which let the skin nodes within two edge rings of such a hexahedron leave the skin by 0.1, 0.25 or 0.5 of a cell while the untangler ran from a fifth of an edge down to 1/1280 of one, then pulled them back under a corner guard, raised the smallest corner four to ten times but hardly the counts, at four to five times the time (`feature_angle=60`, cells with a non-positive corner without and with it): the bunny 2 and 1 (−2e-5 and −4e-7), the Buddha 261 and 219 uniform, 115 and 113 graded, the XYZ RGB dragon 219 and 221 uniform.
- **Why.** The bunny's last cell is a pillow cell at a corner of the grid: its inner node has four neighbours (the grid cell's three and its copy on the skin), and its four corners are positive only inside the tetrahedron they span, which there is flat. No move of the node itself makes it positive, and moving the neighbours trades it against their own cells. The cells left are held by the grid's connectivity, not by the fit.

## Report

`return_report=True` returns the frame (`origin`, `root_size`, `root_cells`, `max_level`, `finest_level`, `surface_level`, `min_size`, `max_size`), the tree (`num_leaves`, `leaves_per_level`), the counts (`num_points`, `num_cells`, `num_hexahedra`, `num_tetrahedra`, `num_inside`, `num_boundary_cells`, `num_surrogate_faces`, `num_hanging`, `num_cut_points`, `num_warped`, `num_warp_refused`), the features (`num_corners`, `num_corners_snapped`, `num_feature_chains`, `num_feature_chains_missed`, `num_feature_moves`), the optimisation or untangling (`num_optimized_moves`, `num_flips`), `guarantee_applies`, the quality (`min_scaled_jacobian`, `min_dihedral`, `max_dihedral`, `num_inverted`, `hausdorff_to_skin` (the two-sided distance between the mesh's boundary and the skin), `num_boundary_edges_open` and `num_non_manifold_boundary_edges` (of the boundary, both 0 for a watertight one), and `quality`, the [`compute_quality`](mesh_quality.md) summary with its histograms), `num_winding_rechecks`, `num_winding_skipped` (points deep inside a closed skin whose winding number was flooded in), `timings` (seconds per stage) and `warnings`. Anything that misses its target is reported in `warnings`, never hidden.

## Determinism

Every leaf and point is an integer coordinate on the finest lattice, deduplicated by sorting, and every parallel loop writes disjoint slots: the output is byte-identical across parallel backends and thread counts.

## Bindings

Python `mp.octree_mesh`, the pipeline step `OctreeMesh`, the CLI verb `octree-mesh` (both CLIs) and the MCP tool `octree_mesh` take the same options; see [pipelines](pipeline.md), [CLI](cli.md) and [MCP](mcp.md). The C API has `mio_octree_mesh` with `mio_octree_mesh_opts` and `mio_octree_mesh_report` ([C API](c_api.md)), and the [Fortran](fortran.md), [Julia](julia.md), [R](r.md) and [WASM](wasm.md) (`octreeMesh`) bindings wrap it. The notebook `example/python/41_octree_mesh.ipynb` walks through every output and draws the figures on this page; `example/python/44_octree_mesh_real_skins.ipynb` meshes the Stanford bunny (graded tetrahedra with its features, the flooded inside test, hexahedra) and a capped cylinder's rims.
