# Changelog

This document records every released version of meshio++ — new formats, new operations,
notable enhancements, and breaking changes. Breaking changes are called out explicitly as
**Breaking:**; everything else is additive unless stated otherwise.

**Keep this file current: add an entry in the same change as every version bump.** See the
"Version bumps" section of `AGENTS.md`.

## Unreleased

- **Added: MMG in the full WebAssembly artifact** ([WebAssembly](doc/wasm.md#mmg-in-the-full-artifact), roadmap 3.1.8): `loadMeshioPlusPlus({}, {full: true})` now links MMG 5.8.0 statically, so `adapt`, `discretizeLevelset` and `meshDomain` run in the browser, with every MMG parameter of the C API's `mio_mmg_params` camelCased and the report the native operations give; `move` is bound and throws naming ELAS, which the artifact leaves out (GPL-3.0); `metricFromHessian` runs in both artifacts; `hasMmg()` is true in the full one and `hasMmgElas()` reports ELAS. The lean artifact stays MMG-free and throws naming the full one. `build/build-wasm-deps.sh --full` builds MMG from its pinned commit (`build/deps-pins.sh`) with one patch (`build/patches/mmg-5.8.0-emscripten.patch`: stub Fortran headers, one initialised tentative definition); CMake accepts the static `Mmg::libmmg_a` for the full artifact only. **The full pair's licence expression adds `LGPL-3.0-or-later`**, and its notices the MMG relinking recipe. With it the operations section of the roadmap closes.
- **Changed: `optimize_volume` raises the worst cell by default** ([optimize_volume](doc/optimize_volume.md), roadmap 3.2.1). The new default objective, `worst`, treats the least dihedral angle as the quality and never lowers it: the ODT relocation is guarded (a vertex whose star got worse goes back), the 2-3 flip is joined by edge removal (the ring of 3 to `max_ring_size` = 7 tets around an interior edge replaced by its best triangulation, by Shewchuk's dynamic program) and multi-face removal (its inverse), and worst-cell smoothing climbs each poor vertex's least angle (Freitag and Ollivier-Gooch 1997; Klingner and Shewchuk 2007). On Netgen's tetrahedra after its own three passes it raises the least angle from 17.4 to 32.4 degrees on a sphere and from 13.3 to 19.6 on a capped cylinder, where the former behaviour lowered it to 12.5 and 8.3. That former behaviour stays as `objective="energy"`, byte for byte; `octree_mesh` and the octree `tetrahedralize` backend keep it, so their output does not move. The report adds `num_edge_removals`, `num_multi_face_removals`, `num_worst_smoothed` and `min_dihedral_before`/`after` on every surface (both CLIs' `--objective`/`--max-ring-size`, the pipeline's `Objective`/`MaxRingSize`, MCP, C `mio_optimize_volume_ex` with `mio_optimize_volume_opts`/`_report`, Fortran, Julia, R, WASM). `optimize_volume`'s default output changes, so its golden digest was re-recorded; `tools/optvol_probe.py` measures it on Netgen's output and `tests/python/test_optimize_volume_netgen.py` pins the guarantee there.
- **Added: `select`** ([select](doc/select.md), roadmap 3.1.3): writes one named point, cell or side region chosen by a box, half-space or ball (each cell or side reduced by `mode` `all`, `any` or `centroid`), a scalar `point_data`/`cell_data` comparison, a quality-metric comparison, a node-connected component (numbered as `split(by="component")` numbers its pieces), or a smooth patch grown from the facet nearest a seed point across the edges `feature_edges` would not draw. Criteria intersect; the mesh is otherwise bit-identical, and a region of the same name and kind is replaced. Cell regions feed `find_interface`, side regions `split_interface` and solver decks. On every surface: Python, both CLIs (`select`), the pipeline (`Select`), MCP (`select`), the C API (`mio_select` with `mio_select_opts` and `mio_select_report`), Fortran and Julia (`select_region`), R (`mio_select`) and WASM (`selectRegion`). Core-only: without the compiled core the Python function raises `NotImplementedError`. The example notebook `49_select.ipynb` shows it.
- `split(by="component")`'s labelling moved into a shared core helper that `select` uses too; its pieces are unchanged.
- **Added: `tet_to_poly`** ([tetrahedral ⇄ polyhedral](doc/tet_poly.md)): the median dual of a tetrahedral mesh, the tet-to-poly conversion CFD meshers perform, with one polyhedron per vertex. Each cell is bounded by the ring of tetrahedron and triangle centroids around each edge leaving its vertex (closed through the edge midpoint at the boundary) and by its share of the boundary triangles. Those pieces are fused per run of triangles whose normals agree within `feature_angle` (default 1 degree), so feature edges and corners survive and, at the default, the volume is conserved. A vertex on an interface between `tetra` blocks yields one cell per block. Each `tetra` block becomes a `polyhedron` block and each `triangle` block a `polygon` block of its dual faces. `cell_data` is volume-weighted (integers take the largest tetrahedron's value) and `point_data` is extended to the new points. `point_data_to_cell_data` and `record_vertex_ids` (`dual:vertex`) are optional.
- **Added: `poly_to_tet`** ([tetrahedral ⇄ polyhedral](doc/tet_poly.md)): `convert_cells(mode="simplexify")`, except that a polyhedron that already is a tetrahedron becomes exactly that one `tetra`, with no new points, so a tetrahedral mesh stored as `polyhedron4` cells comes back exactly. `convert_cells` itself is unchanged.
- Both operations are on every surface: Python, both CLIs (`tet-to-poly`, `poly-to-tet`), the pipeline (`TetToPoly`, `PolyToTet`), MCP, the C API (`mio_tet_to_poly` with `mio_tet_to_poly_opts`, and `mio_poly_to_tet`), Fortran, Julia, R and WASM (`tetToPoly`, `polyToTet`). The example notebook `48_tet_poly.ipynb` shows them.
- **Added: `apply_operations`** ([the settings pipeline](doc/pipeline.md#in-memory-apply-operations), roadmap 7.2.1): the pipeline's steps applied to a mesh in memory, `(mesh, report) = apply_operations(mesh, operations)`, with `run_pipeline`'s vocabulary, validation and report and no Input read or Output write. `operations` is a list of steps, its JSON text or a settings document; a model step meshes the CAD model in `mesh.info`, and `Split` and a fanned-out `Partition` raise naming `run_pipeline`. `run_pipeline` and `apply_operations` share one step loop. Python-only; the MCP server lists it as the in-memory form of its `pipeline` tool.
- **Added: `adjacency` and `write_graph`** ([adjacency graphs](doc/adjacency.md), roadmap 7.2.2): a mesh's graph in CSR form, `(indptr, indices)`, of kind `node` (cell edges, polyhedron faces and uniform `polygon*` rings included), `clique` (nodes sharing a cell: the FEM sparsity pattern) or `cell` (cells sharing a facet: the dual graph `partition`'s KaHIP method cuts), and the METIS `.graph` and Scotch `.grf` files written from one. On every surface: Python (with numpy twins, byte-identical), the C API (`mio_adjacency_create` with zero-copy `mio_adjacency_indptr`/`_indices`, and `mio_write_graph`), Fortran, Julia and R (1-based), WASM (`adjacency`, `writeGraph`), both CLIs (`graph`) and MCP (`graph`). The facet dual graph moved out of `partition.cpp` into a core-private helper the two share; KaHIP's input is unchanged. The new header `operations/adjacency.hpp` is additive ([ABI review](doc/abi_reviews.md)).
- **Added: compressed files read transparently** ([compressed files](doc/formats.md#compressed-files), roadmap 7.1.1): `read("cube.msh.gz")`, `.bz2`, `.xz`, `.zst` and a single-member `.zip` strip the suffix to infer the format, decompress into a private temporary directory under the inner name, read and clean up. Python through the standard library (zstd through Python 3.14, the `zstandard` package of the `codecs` extra, or the core); the native registry through the codecs the build links (`MESHIOPLUSPLUS_WITH_ZLIB`, `_BZIP2`, `_LZMA`, `_ZSTD`, a missing one named in the `ReadError`), so both CLIs, the C API (`mio_read`, `mio_read_ex`, `mio_read_with_info`, `mio_read_metadata_create`), Fortran, Julia, R and WASM's `readMesh` read them with no code of their own. Compound extensions a format owns (`.vol.gz`, `.xda.gz`, `.dato.gz`) are unchanged; multi-file formats and multi-file zips are refused by name. New installed header `detail/compressed_input.hpp`, additive ([ABI review](doc/abi_reviews.md)); `_core.decompress` and `_core.compression_available` expose the native codecs to Python.
- **Added: remote files** ([remote files](doc/formats.md#remote-files), roadmap 7.1.2): with the optional `fsspec` (new `remote` extra), `read`, `read_metadata`, sequences and dataset manifests take URLs (`https://`, `s3://`, `gs://`, `memory://`, chained `simplecache::`...), fetched to a temporary copy under the URL's file name, so format inference and compressed files work unchanged; a `*`/`?` pattern is expanded on the remote filesystem. Python-only; multi-file formats are refused by name, and the MCP server never fetches a URL.
- **Added: third-party formats by entry point** ([extending](doc/extending.md#shipping-a-format-as-a-package), roadmap 7.1.3): a package declaring a `meshioplusplus.formats` entry point (a callable that calls `register_format`, or a module that does on import) adds its format by being installed. Read once on the first use of the format registry; a broken plugin is skipped with a warning naming it (`_plugins.format_plugin_errors()`), and `MESHIOPLUSPLUS_NO_PLUGINS=1` skips discovery.
- **Added: length units as metadata** ([length units](doc/formats.md#length-units), roadmap 7.1.4): `field_data["meshio:length_unit"]` (metres per unit) is filled by the UNV (dataset 164), Radioss (`/BEGIN` work unit) and USD (`metersPerUnit`) readers, honoured by their writers (UNV writes dataset 164 from it, Radioss names it in `/BEGIN`, USD's `meters_per_unit` defaults to it), and updated by `transform(scale_units=...)`, the pipeline's `ScaleUnits` and both CLIs' `--scale-units`; so a UNV file in millimetres converts to a USD stage with `metersPerUnit = 0.001`. Both engines read and write the same bytes. **Changed:** a Radioss deck written from a mesh in millimetres now says `mm` in `/BEGIN` (it said `m`), and reading any deck whose `/BEGIN` names a unit, a UNV file with dataset 164 or a USD stage that authors `metersPerUnit` now adds the `meshio:length_unit` field. New installed header `detail/length_unit.hpp`, additive ([ABI review](doc/abi_reviews.md)).
- **Added: `geodesic_distance`** ([geodesic distance](doc/geodesic.md), roadmap 7.3.1): the distance along a triangle surface from source points (ids and/or a region's points) to every point, as `geodesic:distance` point data, by the heat method of Crane, Weischedel and Wardetzky (2013) over the triangles' cotangent stiffness and lumped mass. The heat step is a Jacobi iteration, accurate relative to values far below a residual-based solver's reach, and the Poisson step Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate gradients; both deterministic. Measured accuracy is tabled on the page (about 1% on a sphere). Core-only with no numpy twin, on every surface: Python, C (`mio_geodesic_distance`, `mio_geodesic_opts`, `mio_geodesic_report`), Fortran, Julia, R, WASM (`geodesicDistance`), both CLIs (`geodesic`), the pipeline (`GeodesicDistance`) and MCP. New header `operations/geodesic.hpp`, additive ([ABI review](doc/abi_reviews.md)).
- **Added: `laplacian_eigenmodes`** ([eigenmodes](doc/eigenmodes.md), roadmap 7.3.2): the lowest eigenpairs of a triangle surface's Laplace–Beltrami operator (cotangent stiffness against lumped mass, Neumann on a boundary) by block shift-inverted subspace iteration with Rayleigh–Ritz, so repeated eigenvalues come out whole: on an icosahedral sphere 0, 2 (three times) and 6 (five times), approaching `l (l + 1)`. As `laplacian:modes` point data (M-orthonormal) and `laplacian:eigenvalues` field data. Core only, on every surface: Python, the pipeline (`LaplacianEigenmodes`, both engines), C (`mio_laplacian_eigenmodes`), Fortran, Julia, R, WASM (`laplacianEigenmodes`), both CLIs (`eigenmodes`, same JSON) and MCP. The surface operators are shared with `geodesic_distance` (its output is byte-identical).
- **Added: `morph`** ([morphing](doc/morph.md), roadmap 7.4.1): boundary-driven mesh morphing. The constrained points (the boundary, a point set or mask) take a prescribed displacement and every other point its harmonic extension over the P1 stiffness of the mesh's top-dimensional cells (every linear type, decomposed into simplices for the assembly), with Jacobian-based stiffening and incremental steps; afterwards an inversion guard compares every cell's orientation with the input's and refuses a result with inverted cells unless allowed. Lifting a hexahedral box's top face by 0.1 inverts nothing; a 90° rotation of an annulus's inner circle stays valid with stiffening in 4 steps where plain Laplace inverts 48 cells. Core only, on every surface: Python, the pipeline (`Morph`, both engines), C (`mio_morph`), Fortran, Julia, R, WASM (`morph`), both CLIs (`morph`, same JSON) and MCP.
- **Added: `untangle`** ([untangling](doc/untangle.md), roadmap 7.4.2): repairs inverted tetrahedra and hexahedra by node-by-node descent on Escobar et al.'s untangling and smoothing functional (a regularised signed volume over every corner simplex, Knupp's corner Jacobians for hexahedra), keeping the connectivity and the boundary; only nodes within a few rings of a tangle move unless `smooth`. A tetrahedral cube with a node pushed through a face ends with every signed volume positive; on a heavily jittered one it leaves no inverted cell where `smooth` leaves 19, without `optimize_volume`'s edge flips, and it handles hexahedra. Core only, on every surface: Python, the pipeline (`Untangle`, both engines), C (`mio_untangle`), Fortran, Julia, R, WASM (`untangle`), both CLIs (`untangle`, same JSON) and MCP.
- **Fixed: `geodesic_distance` stopped short on tiny surfaces.** The automatic iteration cap, twice the point count plus 10, was too low for the Gauss–Seidel heat step on a handful of points (a cube's 8 needed 65 sweeps; the cap floored at 64), which reported `converged: false`; it is now at least 1000. Converged results are unchanged.
- **Added: shell completions** ([CLI](doc/cli.md#shell-completions), roadmap 7.6.2): `meshioplusplus completion bash|zsh|fish` in both CLIs prints a completion script generated from the CLI's own parsers (the Python argparse tree; the native verbs' `cli_parse` option tables, read through a describe mode), completing verbs and aliases, the `data`/`dataset` sub-verbs, options and file names.
- **Added: `convert_coordinates`** ([coordinates](doc/coordinates.md), roadmap 7.6.1): positions, and vector and tensor fields (point or cell data, `(n, 3)`, `(n, 9)` or `(n, 3, 3)`), in cylindrical or spherical components about an axis, recorded as `coords:cylindrical`/`coords:spherical` point data, with an inverse back to Cartesian components; points on the axis are reported as singular. Core and numpy twin agree to rounding. On every surface: Python, the pipeline (`ConvertCoordinates`, both engines), C (`mio_convert_coordinates`, `mio_coordinate_opts`), Fortran, Julia, R, WASM (`convertCoordinates`), both CLIs (`coordinates`, same JSON) and MCP.
- **Added: `convex_hull` and `delaunay_2d`** ([hull](doc/hull.md), roadmap 7.5.1): the convex hull of a point set (quickhull in 3-D with coplanar faces merged and re-triangulated from their corners, so only extreme points are vertices; Andrew's monotone chain in 2-D) as a closed outward `triangle` surface or a `line` loop carrying `hull:point_id`, and the Delaunay triangulation of the points' `x, y` (the planar constrained triangulation with the hull edges as constraints) keeping every point, its `z` and its data. Every decision is an exact predicate. Core only, on every surface: Python, the pipeline (`ConvexHull`, `Delaunay2D`, both engines), C (`mio_convex_hull` with `mio_hull_report`, `mio_delaunay_2d`), Fortran, Julia, R, WASM (`convexHull`, `delaunay2d`), both CLIs (`hull`, `delaunay`, same JSON) and MCP.
- **Added: `estimate_normals`** ([point-cloud normals](doc/point_normals.md), roadmap 7.5.2, first part): normals of a point cloud from a plane fitted to each point's `k` nearest neighbours (the least eigenvector of their covariance), oriented by Hoppe et al.'s minimum spanning tree propagation and then outward per connected piece, towards a viewpoint, or canonically; written as `normals` point data (the PCD and XYZ normal columns), with an optional `normals:variation`. Core only, on every surface: Python, the pipeline (`EstimateNormals`, both engines), C (`mio_estimate_normals`, `mio_point_normals_opts`), Fortran, Julia, R, WASM (`estimateNormals`), both CLIs (`point-normals`, same JSON) and MCP.
- **Added: `register_rigid`** ([rigid registration](doc/registration.md), roadmap 7.5.3): rigid registration by iterative closest points, onto a target's surface (closest points on its cells) or a point cloud (nearest points), point-to-point (Horn's quaternion closed form) or point-to-plane (linearised, applied as an exact rotation; a cloud target needs `normals`), with outlier rejection by distance and an initial transform; returns the 4 × 4 matrix `transform` applies. A rotated and shifted copy registers back within 1e-9. Core only, on every surface: Python, the pipeline (`RegisterRigid`, both engines), C (`mio_register_rigid`), Fortran, Julia, R, WASM (`registerRigid`), both CLIs (`register`, same JSON) and MCP.
- **ABI:** `MESHIOPLUSPLUS_ABI_VERSION` 36 -> 37 (Tier A): `OptimizeVolumeOptions` appends `mObjective` and `mMaxRingSize` (40 -> 48 bytes) and `OptimizeVolumeResult` five fields; the new headers `operations/tet_poly.hpp` and `operations/select.hpp` ride along. See [ABI](doc/abi.md).
- **Fixed: Gmsh 4.0 and 4.1 readers refuse cell data shorter than its elements.** The C++ reader read past the end of the array (a heap-buffer-overflow the fuzz readers shard found); both readers now raise `ReadError`. The Python reference reader also raises `ReadError`, not a bare `ValueError`, for a malformed data-section header or a non-numeric value.
- **Fixed: HDF5 readers refuse attributes that do not hold one value of their type's size.** The attribute readers copied past the end of an attribute whose stored bytes disagreed with its type and extent, which HDF5 2.2.0 does not check either (found by the fuzz readers shard in an HMF file). Such files now raise `ReadError`. The campaign's HDF5 pin stays at 1.14.6.

## v18.0.0 (2026-10-30)

- **Breaking: Python 3.8 and 3.9 are no longer supported** (`requires-python = ">=3.10"`, also for the three add-on wheels): the CI runner image (Ubuntu 26.04) has no Python 3.9, and the interop and MCP extras already needed 3.10. Python 3.13 and 3.14 are added to the tested versions and the wheels (`cp310` to `cp314`).
- **Breaking: OpenCASCADE, Netgen and MMG move out of the PyPI base wheel into `meshioplusplus-occt`, `-netgen` and `-mmg`** (roadmap 4.7, [installation](doc/installation.md#what-the-pypi-wheels-contain), [split-package plan](doc/python_packages_plan.md#what-shipped-in-v18-0-0)). The base wheel, `meshioplusplus`, now carries no copyleft code (it is `MIT AND MPL-2.0`, the MPL being Eigen's). STEP and IGES reading and `fit_cad`'s OpenCASCADE backend need the add-on wheel: `pip install "meshioplusplus[occt]"` (also installed by `[addons]`). It pins the base wheel's exact version, ships OpenCASCADE 7.6.3 as shared libraries beside its own extension (`MIT AND LGPL-2.1-only WITH OCCT-exception-1.0`), and registers its kernel when `meshioplusplus` first needs one. Without it, reading STEP or IGES raises `NotImplementedError` naming the add-on, and `fit_cad` uses its native backend. `MESHIOPLUSPLUS_NO_PLUGINS=1` skips the discovery, and an add-on of another version is refused by name. Source builds, the native CLI, Conan, vcpkg and both WebAssembly artifacts are unchanged: they compile OpenCASCADE into the core as before.
- **Added: a runtime provider registry** ([`providers.hpp`](doc/python_packages_plan.md#the-provider-registry-core)): `CadKernelProvider`, `TetMesherProvider` and `RemesherProvider`, each with `register_*`, `unregister_*` and an accessor (`cad_kernel`, `tet_mesher`, `remesher`), and `register_builtin_providers`. `tetrahedralize` asks the mesher per closed region and `adapt`, `discretize_levelset`, `mesh_domain` and `move` ask the remesher; the Netgen and MMG code moved to `src/cpp/providers/{netgen,mmg}`. `read_step`, `read_iges`, `fit_cad` and the capability queries (`cad_read_has_occt`, `fit_cad_has_occt`, `fit_cad_occt_version`) ask it instead of a compile-time switch. The OpenCASCADE code moved to `src/cpp/providers/occt/` and reaches the native `fit_cad` helpers through `detail/fit_cad_native.hpp`.
- **Added: the shared core in the base wheel.** `MESHIOPLUSPLUS_PYTHON_SHARED_CORE=ON` links `_core` against `libmeshioplusplus_core_meshio` installed in `meshioplusplus/lib` with the headers and the CMake package, and `meshioplusplus.get_cmake_dir()` returns the package directory. The Linux wheels keep a static libstdc++ with its symbols exported, which lets exceptions cross between the core and an add-on. `MESHIOPLUSPLUS_ADDON=occt` builds only the add-on's extension against an installed base wheel, and `build/build-occt.sh PREFIX --shared` builds the shared OpenCASCADE it links.
- **Added: the add-on wheels `meshioplusplus-netgen` and `meshioplusplus-mmg`** ([installation](doc/installation.md#what-the-pypi-wheels-contain)): `pip install "meshioplusplus[netgen]"` gives `tetrahedralize` its Netgen backend, `"[mmg]"` gives `adapt`, `discretize_levelset` and `mesh_domain` (the wheel is built without ELAS, so `move` raises naming it). `has_netgen()` is public beside `has_mmg()` and `has_occt()`, the MCP `tetrahedralize` tool reports `netgen_available`, and `build/build-netgen.sh` builds Netgen 6.2.2401 shared.
- **Added: the permissive libraries in the base wheel on Linux and macOS** (roadmap 4.7): HDF5, netCDF, cgnslib, zstd, LZ4, bzip2 and KaHIP are linked static with hidden symbols (`build/build-permissive-libs.sh`, `MESHIOPLUSPLUS_PREFER_STATIC_DEPS`), so the HDF5, netCDF and CGNS formats and the codecs run natively in the wheel and h5py or netCDF4 can sit beside them in one process. The Windows wheels keep the Python fallbacks.
- **Added: provider plugins for the native CLI and the C API** ([C++ API](doc/cpp_api.md#providers-and-plugins), not on Windows): `MESHIOPLUSPLUS_PLUGIN_PATH`, `load_plugin`, `load_plugins`, `mio_load_plugins` (with Fortran, Julia and R wrappers) and `-DMESHIOPLUSPLUS_BUILD_PLUGINS=ON`, which builds the OpenCASCADE, Netgen and MMG providers as shared libraries. `meshioplusplus --version` lists the backends a run has.
- **Changed: `wheels.yml`** builds the base wheel without OpenCASCADE and a `build_occt_addon` job per runner from that wheel, tests the pair with `tests/wheel_occt_smoke.py` and publishes `meshioplusplus-occt` as its own PyPI project after the base wheel. The Windows core DLL takes its export table from `cmake/gen_core_def.py` (auto-export overflows the 65,535-export limit). The repair steps for the pair, and the PyPI trusted publisher for the new project, are first exercised by the release run.
- **ABI:** pure addition (`providers.hpp`, plus the plugin functions declared there), `MESHIOPLUSPLUS_ABI_VERSION` stays 36; see [ABI reviews](doc/abi_reviews.md).

- **Added: `find_intersections`** (roadmap 3.1.2, [intersections](doc/find_intersections.md)): where a surface intersects itself, or where two surfaces interpenetrate or touch. Candidate triangle pairs come from a bucket grid over their boxes and each is decided by the exact `orient3d`/`orient2d` triangle–triangle test the boundary layer already used, so every hit is exact and there is no "possible" class; on one surface, triangles sharing corners (bitwise-equal coordinates, so unwelded seams are not crossings) count only when they meet beyond them. Returns the input with `intersections:count` per cell, the sorted cell pairs and the crossing segments as a line mesh (`intersections:cell_a`/`cell_b`); coplanar overlaps are counted and flagged without a segment. On every surface: Python, C (`mio_find_intersections` and its opaque result), Fortran, Julia, R, WASM, both CLIs (`find-intersections`, exit 1 on a hit), MCP and a `FindIntersections` pipeline gate.
- **Added: `thickness`** (roadmap 3.1.4, [thickness](doc/thickness.md)): wall thickness and clearance per facet, by a ray from each cell's centroid along its normal through a bounding-volume hierarchy of the target surface: inward it stops at the far side of the wall, outward at the next wall facing it, on the same surface or on a second part. Returns the input with `thickness:distance` (NaN for a miss) and `thickness:hit_cell`, and the least, largest and mean distance with where the least was measured; `threshold` counts the thin facets. The ray–triangle kernel is factored out of `tri_bvh_ray` once (`detail::ray_triangle`, with a filtered cast beside it) and its existing callers are unchanged. On every surface: Python, C (`mio_thickness`), Fortran, Julia, R, WASM, both CLIs (`thickness`, `--min` exits 1 below it), MCP and a `Thickness` pipeline step.
- **Added: Netgen in the full WebAssembly artifact** ([lean and full builds](doc/wasm.md#lean-and-full-builds), [tetrahedralize](doc/tetrahedralize.md)): `meshioplusplus_wasm_full[_mt]` links Netgen 6.2.2401's nglib statically, built from the pinned tag by `build/build-wasm-deps.sh --full` with one build patch, so `tetrahedralize` in the browser keeps the surface's triangles and points (`hasNetgen()` is true there). The lean artifact stays Netgen-free; its `backend: 'netgen'` now names the full artifact instead of a CMake flag. The full pair's licence expression gains Netgen's LGPL-2.1 (`MIT AND (LGPL-2.1-only WITH OCCT-exception-1.0) AND LGPL-2.1-only`). KaHIP's tarball also falls back to a commit-verified clone of its tag, as netCDF's and CGNS's do.
